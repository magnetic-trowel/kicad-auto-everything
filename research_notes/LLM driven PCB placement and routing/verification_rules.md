# Verifying the numbers in the rules catalogs against primary sources (rules_highspeed_emi.md and rules_power_rf_analog_dfm.md)

> **Method and provenance.** This pass re-checked the numeric rules in two catalogs: `rules_highspeed_emi.md` (IDs RP, DP, TL, XT, EMI, plus the myth table) and sections 1–8 of `rules_power_rf_analog_dfm.md` (IDs PWR, PDN, RF, AMS, TH, DFM). The checks used the primary documents themselves, read in full or in their relevant sections, not search snippets. PDFs were downloaded with curl or wget through the session proxy and converted with `pdftotext -layout`. Page numbers below are **PDF page numbers**; where the printed folio differs, that is noted (for example SLYT614 PDF p.1 = AAJ p.11).
>
> **Access notes.**
> - `analog.com` refused curl and Python but **served PDFs to wget** (MT-031, MT-097, MT-101, Kueck articles, and Analog Dialogue 50-02). Its HTML pages and the AN-136 PDF returned 403/404.
> - AN-139 was read from a verbatim mirror of the Linear Technology original.
> - ST AN2867 Rev 19 was read from ECS Inc.'s verbatim copy, because st.com refused.
> - Würth ANP047 was read as the author's republication in Power Systems Design, because we-online.com returned 403.
> - Nordic guidance was read from the nRF52832 Product Specification v1.0 (SparkFun mirror), because Nordic's own sites returned 403.
> - EDN (Bogatin's numbered rules of thumb), Clemson `cecas.clemson.edu` (TLS chain not trusted by the proxy), NXP AN10798/AN1902 (404), the KiCad KLC site (403) and paywalled IPC standards could not be read. Rules that depend only on those sources are marked **NOT RETRIEVABLE**.
> - Interface-specific numbers (USB, PCIe, DDR, Ethernet, HDMI, MIPI, crystal and buck numeric gaps) belong to `interface_rule_packs.md`. They appear here only where a catalog rule ID cites them.
>
> **Status legend.**
> - **VERIFIED**: the number and its meaning match the source.
> - **VERIFIED+CTX**: the number matches, but the catalog dropped a condition that matters.
> - **CORRECTED**: the source gives a different value, attribution or meaning.
> - **CONTESTED**: primary sources disagree.
> - **NOT FOUND**: absent from the primary document(s) read.
> - **NOT RETRIEVABLE**: the source could not be fetched.

## 1. Verification table: catalog rule → claimed value → verified value and status → source and page

### Takeaway
Most P0/P1 and S1/S2 numbers that trace to a named vendor application note are correct. These include TI SPRAAR7's 1.5 W void clearance, 200 mil stitching vias, 5 W pair spacing, <15 mil via stubs and the 5/50 mil skew tables; AN-139's plane-under-hot-loop data; SLYT614's CIN-first, no-vias, one-via-per-amp and single-point-ground rules; the 8 dB inductor-dot result; JLCPCB's limits; and IPC-7351's 0.10/0.25/0.50 mm courtyard excess. Many of them, however, lost a context condition, as follows.
- **Espressif's 15 mm** is an *in-enclosure* antenna clearance, not a PCB copper keep-out.
- **TI's numbers** are tied to specific TI SoC families. For example, PCIe/SATA/HDMI pairs are allowed **0** vias on AM57xx/KeyStone II but 2 on AM64x/AM65x.
- **TI SLVA680 gives no millimetre distance for the TVS.**

Corrections:
- The guard-trace paper is by **Bogatin and Simonovich**, and it recommends end-shorted, length-matched guards in stripline only. It does *not* recommend λ/10 stitching, and it says never to use a guard in microstrip.
- **C_boot is ≥10× C_g** (a minimum), not ≈10×.
- The **IPC-2221B >500 V B4 rate is 0.00305 mm/V**, not 0.005.
- **JLCPCB's silkscreen minimum is 1.0 mm text height and 0.15 mm stroke.**
- The **u-blox "≤0.5 mm / ≤2 mm via" numbers were not found** in the three u-blox manuals read.

Several "myths" are genuinely contested by primary sources:
- **20-H rule:** Montrose et al. call it "fact, not fiction" for high-density multilayer boards.
- **CIN–COUT ground separation:** Rohm says 1–2 cm, MPS says close.
- **Copper under the inductor:** Rohm and TI disagree.
- **Stripline versus microstrip for high-speed pairs:** TI SPRAAR7 and Ott disagree.
- **Tenting thermal vias:** TI SLOA122 says don't; TI SLUA271 and Renesas TB389 say tent from the top.

### Inferences
- Rules whose numbers came from **TI SPRAAR7, TI SLYT614/SLVA773/SLVA680, ADI AN-139/MT-031/MT-101/MT-097, ST AN2867, EPC, Würth ANP047, JLCPCB and IPC-7351 (via Hausherr)** held up well. Almost every discrepancy is a **dropped applicability condition** (device family, data rate, enclosure vs PCB, single measured part, E-field only), not a wrong number. The catalogs' search-snippet method therefore produced mostly correct values with incomplete scopes. A rule record therefore needs explicit `applies_if` and `source_scope` fields.
- **Blog-only mm thresholds** were never confirmed by a primary source. These are: TVS ≤5–10 mm, crystal ≤5 mm, CIN gap ≤0.5–1 mm, RF–SMPS 20 mm, stitching 3–5 mm, flying-probe numbers and 2 cm²/W. The primary sources use relative language ("as near as design rules allow", "much further than") or a looser number (MPS: CIN <2 mm). These should become **ranked objectives**, not DRC limits.
- Several catalog "gaps" are closable with primary numbers:
  - HS-trace-to-edge: 90 mil from the reference-plane edge (TI), or ≥2× trace height from the board edge (LearnEMC).
  - Orthogonal adjacent signal layers: TI and Ott.
  - G/S/S/G 4-layer: Ott.
  - Ott's 6-layer ordering.
  - Shunt tap point: onsemi, centre of the inner pad edge.
  - Attribution of the 1/6-tr threshold: Johnson.
- Where two primary vendors disagree, they usually optimise different things:
  - Rohm (CIN/COUT ground separation, no ground under the inductor) targets conducted HF noise and inductor Q. TI and MPS target loop area and shielding.
  - TI SPRAAR7 says no stripline for HS pairs, plausibly to avoid via stubs on its SoC reference boards. Ott says bury HS signals for shielding.
  - These are context switches, so the rule engine should store both, with the condition that selects between them.

### Gaps
- **Bogatin EDN rules of thumb #1, #17, #20 and #21 (2.5% NEXT at 2 W; FEXT 0.5% × Len/RT) could not be read.** EDN refused curl and wget, and the Internet Archive was blocked. XT-1 and XT-2 remain unverified. The colorado.edu index confirms only the titles.
- **Hubing EMCS03-001, Shim & Hubing 20-H measurements and EMCS97-159** (cecas.clemson.edu) failed TLS verification: the chain ends at an eMudhra root that is not in the proxy bundle. The "3.6 dB at TM10" figure remains unverified.
- **ADI AN-136**: the PDF URL now redirects to an HTML page that returned 403/404. AN-136-specific claims (a ground layer between the power-stage and small-signal layers; gate-drive loop guidance) were verified only through the search snippet already in the catalog. AN-139 and the Kueck articles support the plane-shielding principle.
- **ADI "Layout for Precision Op Amps", Microchip leakage page, Nordic DevZone pages, KiCad KLC F5.3, NXP AN10798/AN1902 and Würth's own ANP047 PDF**: all returned 403/404. Alternatives were used where noted.
- **u-blox "≤0.5 mm / ≤2 mm" via rule**: not found in three u-blox manuals. It may exist in another module's integration manual (for example SARA or NINA), which was not checked.
- IPC-2221/2152/7351 are paywalled. The values come from expert or vendor reproductions and arithmetic.
- Not re-checked against manufacturer primaries (all remain secondary as in the catalog): MLCC DC-bias derating figures, via ampacity (1.9 A / 1.6 A), per-via thermal resistance, tombstoning numbers, V-score MLCC distance (3–5 mm), panel tab dimensions and target-impedance examples.

## 2. Notable additions: important rules from the same primary sources that the catalogs missed

### Takeaway
The primary documents contain many precise, automatable numbers that the catalogs did not capture. The most valuable are:
- TI SPRAAR7's absolute keep-outs: 30 mil to any signal, 50 mil to clocks, 90 mil from the reference-plane edge, 30 mil anti-pads, ≥2-layer-deep voids under SMD pads, 60% voids under ESD pads, breakout within 0.25 in, and bends >135°.
- SLVA680's ESD-path rules: ≤45° corners and one via immediately adjacent to the TVS ground pin.
- LearnEMC's 2 cm I/O-zone and 2×h edge rules.
- Ott's stack-up spacings.
- Espressif's crystal, RF and power numbers.
- Nordic's VSS-pin shunt grounding and inner-layer keep-outs.
- MT-031's output-isolation and ground-voltage limits.
- Archambeault's depth-to-plane inductance table.
- JLCPCB's V-cut 0.4 mm and assembly-panel geometry.

### Cited Findings

#### High-speed / SI (TI SPRAAR7J, Rev. J, Feb 2023; NXP AN10373)
- Absolute keep-outs: "maintain a minimum keep-out area of 30 mils to any other signal throughout the length of the trace… abut a clock or a periodic signal, increase this keep-out to a minimum of 50 mils"; pairs not "within 30 mils of another differential pair after package escape". The tables repeat these as "Differential pair to clock or high-speed periodic signal trace spacing 50 Mils; … to any other signal trace spacing 30 Mils". — [TI SPRAAR7J p.9–10, p.16–20](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- "Ensure that high-speed differential signals are routed ≥ 90 mils from the edge of the reference plane." — [TI SPRAAR7J p.9](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- "Do not route high-speed traces under or near crystals, oscillators, clock signal generators, switching power regulators, mounting holes, magnetic devices, or ICs that use or duplicate clock signals." After BGA breakout, keep HS pairs "clear of the SoC". — [TI SPRAAR7J p.9](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- "When possible, route high-speed differential pair signals on the top or bottom layer of the PCB with an adjacent GND layer. **TI does not recommend stripline routing of the high-speed differential signals.**" This conflicts with Ott's objective 4 (bury HS signals); see section 3. — [TI SPRAAR7J p.9](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7); [Ott Part 1](https://hott.shielddigitaldesign.com/techtips/pcb-stack-up-1.html)
- Breakout and symmetry: "package break-out must occur within 0.25 inches of the package"; keep a constant trace width after the BGA escape. — [TI SPRAAR7J p.9–10](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- Anti-pads: "as large as possible (30 mils provides significant benefits…)… must be met on all layers where the via exists… non-functional or unconnected via pads are not permitted." Equal via count on P and N, "as equally spaced as possible"; vias "as close as possible to the SoC". — [TI SPRAAR7J p.12](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- In-line SMD pads: "voiding the SMD mounting pads of the reference plane by 100%. This void should be at least two PCB layers deep." For ESD/EMI parts: "Incorporate 60% voids under the ESD/EMI component signal pads"; "Use 0402 0-Ω resistors for common-mode filter (CMF) no-stuff options"; "Place any required signal pair AC coupling capacitors on the protected side of the CMF and as close as possible to the CMF." — [TI SPRAAR7J p.13, p.15](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- Stack-up: "TI recommends a PCB of at least six layers"; "Route directly adjacent signal layers at a 90° offset to each other"; split planes allowed "depending on specific board considerations. Ensure that traces on adjacent planes do not cross splits." Planes that do not reference each other must not overlap (unwanted capacitance). — [TI SPRAAR7J p.8, p.14](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- Fiber weave: rotate the image or the HS routing "10° to 35°" relative to the weave, or zig-zag; prefer tighter weaves (Fig. 2-4 contrasts styles 1080, 2116 and 7628). NXP: route long traces "at an off-angle to the X-Y axis". — [TI SPRAAR7J p.3–5](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7); [NXP AN10373 p.4](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/powerquicc/2284/2/philips_PCIe_pcb_routing_guide.pdf)
- Through-hole receptacles: connect HS pairs on the **bottom** layer so the TH pin is not a stub; SMT receptacles on top (no vias). — [TI SPRAAR7J p.10](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- Device tables cap total trace length, for example USB3 3500 mil, PCIe 4700 mil and HDMI 4000 mil on AM57xx, and USB2 12000 mil. PCIe impedance in these TI tables is **100 Ω ±10%** (AM57xx/KeyStone) or **95 Ω ±5%** (AM64x/AM65x), not 85 Ω. Interface details are left to `interface_rule_packs.md`. — [TI SPRAAR7J p.16–19](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)
- NXP AN10373 (2005 FR-4): trace adds "1 ps to 5 ps of jitter and 0.35 dB to 0.50 dB of loss per inch"; add-in card ≤4 in, system board <12 in; pair spacing to other signals "20 mils or four times the dielectric height, whichever is greater", 30 mil if the aggressor has higher voltage or faster edges. — [NXP AN10373 p.4](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/powerquicc/2284/2/philips_PCIe_pcb_routing_guide.pdf)
- Stubs at very high rates: "acceptable stub lengths are now in the 10 mil range (=0.3/30 Gbps)"; "50 mils is too long for even an 8 Gbps stub". — [Telian, SIJ 2023](https://www.signalintegrityjournal.com/articles/2962-fixing-the-problem-of-stubs)
- Termination conservative variant: MT-097's "2 inch (PCB track length)/nanosecond (rise/fall time)" rule. A 2 in microstrip on Er = 4 has a delay of "~270 ps". — [ADI MT-097 p.1](https://www.analog.com/media/en/training-seminars/tutorials/MT-097.pdf)
- Guard traces (if used at all), from the conclusions: "In stripline, never terminate the ends of the guard trace or leave them floating. The ends should be shorted with the lowest inductance via practical"; "match the length of the guard trace to the coupled region"; "Any extension of the guard trace outside the coupling region … will reduce the effectiveness"; "3D simulation is the only way to quantify". — [Bogatin & Simonovich p.32–33](https://www.signalintegrityjournal.com/ext/resources/article-images-2019/Guard-Traces---Love-Them-or-Leave-Them/4567Guard_Trace_DesignCon-2013-rev14_Final.pdf)

#### EMC / ESD (TI SLVA680A, LearnEMC, Hubing, Ott)
- ESD path geometry: between the ESD source and the TVS, "If corners are necessary, they should be curved with the largest radii possible, with 45° corners being the maximum angle"; treat that region as a "Keep-Out area for unprotected PCB traces", including adjacent to any via on that path. — [TI SLVA680A p.3, p.5–6](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/slva680)
- TVS ground: "connect to a same layer ground plane that is coupled with another ground plane on an immediately adjacent layer… with one VIA immediately adjacent to the ground pin of the TVS". The example uses 4 stitching vias and a chassis screw near the TVS. BGA-TVS example via: 0.254 mm pad, 0.152 mm drill, epoxy-filled and plated over, 0.1 mm clearance. Make GND via pad and drill "as large as possible"; use non-conductive fill; keep clearance to non-ground planes minimal. — [TI SLVA680A p.7–8](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/slva680)
- If a via is unavoidable, route "directly from the ESD Source to the TVS before using the VIA" (Case 1). Case 2 (the IC branch before the via to the TVS) "should be avoided". — [TI SLVA680A p.6–7](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/slva680)
- LearnEMC good guidelines with numbers:
  - I/O devices within ~2 cm of their connector; non-I/O components ≥2 cm from I/O nets and connectors.
  - Critical nets ≥2× (trace-to-return distance) from the board edge.
  - Same-layer power planes ≥3 mm apart ("Maybe"), and at least the plane-to-ground spacing.
  - "On a board with power and ground planes, no traces should be used to connect to power or ground. Connections should be made using a via adjacent to the power or ground pad."
  - Minimise via count on HF nets.
  - Tie unused digital inputs high or low.
  — [LearnEMC Good Guidelines](https://learnemc.com/other-good-emc-design-guidelines)
- LearnEMC not-so-good list, useful as **anti-rules**:
  - "Above 25 MHz, all boards should have two ground planes" (wrong).
  - Ground islands under ICs ("a bad idea").
  - "Nets driven at a faster than 1V/ns slew rate must have a discrete series resistor" (slew rate is not the criterion).
  - "Capacitively-loaded nets must have a total source impedance … one-quarter of the line characteristic impedance" ("never").
  - "All power and ground traces must be at least three times the nominal signal line width".
  - "Additional decoupling capacitors … on both sides of a power or ground plane gap" ("Don't gap the ground plane!").
  - "Critical nets should be routed in a daisy chain fashion with no stubs" ("Sometimes, yes. Sometimes, no.").
  - "Ground in at least two places" for E-field shields ("terrible advice").
  — [LearnEMC Not-So-Good](https://learnemc.com/not-so-good-emc-design-guidelines)
- Hubing 2019, seven common errors: blindly following guidelines; uncontrolled transition times; not following currents; not identifying critical traces; more than one "ground"; improper decoupling; inadvertently bypassing filters. Bonding: on boards near metal structure, "Connect to it! That connection is your board ground"; "Bond (at RF) everything that leaves the board or is electrically large". — [Hubing 2019 slides 3, 15–16](https://r6.ieee.org/scv-emcs/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/022019ToddHubing.pdf)
- LearnEMC: SMPS transition times longer than "about 1% of the switching period can noticeably reduce the efficiency". — [LearnEMC PCB Layout](https://learnemc.com/pcb-layout)
- Ott quantitative stack-up facts:
  - "a four-layer board will produce 15 dB less radiation than a two-layer board" (rule of thumb).
  - "Above about ten or fifteen MHz, multi-layer boards should normally be considered".
  - Plane-pair capacitance is insufficient "below about 500 MHz", so prefer tight signal-to-plane coupling.
  - Typical 6-layer spacing "0.005"/0.005"/0.040"/0.005"/0.005"" (0.060" board).
  - 8-layer Fig. 11 spacing "0.006"/0.006"/0.015"/0.006"/0.015"/0.006"/0.006"".
  - "An eight-layer board with six routing layers is definitely not recommended… you should be using a ten-layer board."
  - "There is very little EMC advantage to use a board with more than eight layers."
  — [Ott Parts 1, 3, 4](https://hott.shielddigitaldesign.com/techtips/pcb-stack-up-1.html)
- Ott on ≥14-bit converters: "divide your board into separate isolated analog and digital ground plane regions, each solidly connected to the digital ground plane under each of the A/D converters… no traces, on any layer, can cross over the isolating slots". He also tabulates LSB vs bits at 1 V reference (12-bit 240 µV, 16-bit 15 µV). — [Ott](https://hott.shielddigitaldesign.com/techtips/split-gnd-plane.html)

#### Power conversion (TI, Rohm, MPS, Renesas, EPC, ADI)
- SLYT614: place system vias "outside the circuit, versus between the input capacitor and IC"; quiet-component grounds "are best routed directly back to the AGND pin" rather than via'd into the system plane; "Keep all components on a single side of the PCB"; EN/PG digital parts "are the least important … do these last". — [TI SLYT614 PDF p.2–3](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slyt614/slyt614.pdf)
- AN-139/Kueck: the INTVCC (driver-supply) decoupling loop is a second hot loop with "EMI energy … on the order of 20dB lower than in the main hot loop". Make it small and shield it with a solid plane on the next layer. — [AN-139 p.6](https://theengineer.markallengroup.com/production/content/uploads/2015/01/an139f.pdf); [Kueck Part 2](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/technical-articles/Kueck_Layout_power_supply_part_2.pdf)
- EPC loop inductance targets: 0.4 nH vs 2.9 nH changes efficiency by >4% at 1 MHz/12 V→1.2 V. The optimal layout gave 0.8% and 1% full-load gains over the vertical and lateral loops. Si MOSFET benchmark loop ≈2 nH vs ≈1 nH for eGaN. — [EPC WP010 p.2, p.5](https://epc-co.com/epc/Portals/0/epc/documents/papers/Optimizing%20PCB%20Layout%20with%20eGaN%20FETs.pdf)
- Rohm (60AN066E Rev.004, Jan 2024): CIN and the freewheel diode on the same surface as the IC; do not move CBYPASS to the far side through vias ("bypass capacitor operates as a reverse effect"); do not place the freewheel diode on the bottom layer through vias. — [Rohm p.1, p.3](https://fscdn.rohm.com/en/products/databook/applinote/ic/power/switching_regulator/converter_pcb_layout_appli-e.pdf)
- MPS AN065: VCC cap "<2mm distance from the Chip"; AGND–PGND connection "close to the decoupling capacitor"; thermal vias "10mil_drill/20mil_copper" near the IN and GND pads. — [MPS AN065 p.4–7](https://media.monolithicpower.com/document/PCB_Layout_Design_Guidelines_for_NB67xSeries.pdf)
- Renesas DA9080 (PMIC): input caps "on the same layer as the DA9080 device"; layer 2 as power-GND return, with "GND 'cuts' … between each buck" to limit ground bounce into the LDO and between bucks. This is a vendor-mandated partition that conflicts with the one-ground doctrine. Transfer output current "at the top layer directly without using any vias". — [Renesas DA9080 AN p.8–11](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/apn/da9080-pcb-layout-recommendations)
- TPS62130 land pattern: "It is recommended that vias under paste be filled, plugged or tented." — [TI TPS62130 DS p.41](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps62130.pdf)
- TI SLUAAM3 (GaN drivers): two VDD bypass caps, the smaller closest to the IC; boot cap close to the HB/HS pins; input-side RC components close to the LI/HI pins. — [TI SLUAAM3 p.1, p.3–4](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/sluaam3)

#### PDN / decoupling / mixed-signal (ADI, Archambeault, Bogatin)
- MT-101: the conventional bulk/local scheme (above). Resonance: "0.1 µF and 1 nH will resonate at 16 MHz"; damp with "a small resistance (~10 Ω) in the power line close to the IC" or a ferrite bead, which "appears resistive above 100 kHz". — [ADI MT-101 p.2, p.7–8](https://www.analog.com/media/en/training-seminars/tutorials/MT-101.pdf)
- Archambeault 2012 (IEEE EMC chapter):
  - Mounting-inductance tables (above).
  - On a 10" wide plane, cap-to-IC distance 10" → 545 pH vs 1" → 154 pH, and narrow planes increase inductance sharply.
  - "Should avoid all long thin power/ground structures".
  - "If placed near each other, capacitors should alternate power/ground pins".
  - "When plane pair is deep in PCB stackup, effective inductance is higher".
  — [Archambeault p.61–68](https://ewh.ieee.org/r3/enc/emcs/archive/2012-10-10b_DecouplingMyths.pdf)
- Bogatin, Smith & Sandler: "a 47 uF electrolytic capacitor might have as much as 30 nH of ESL, while a small 0.1 uF disc capacitor might have an ESL as low as 7 nH". The series-RLC model holds "when the mounting inductance is larger than about 1 nH". — [SIJ 2020](https://www.signalintegrityjournal.com/articles/1589-the-myth-of-three-capacitor-values)
- MT-031 additions:
  - "DC or ac voltages of more than 300 mV between the two ground planes can not only damage ICs but cause false triggering … and possible latchup". Back-to-back Schottky diodes between separate AGND and DGND planes at the star.
  - "approximately 0.2-pF unavoidable stray capacitance between every pin of the IC package".
  - Buffer register adjacent to the converter outputs with series R. The worked example uses 500 Ω × 10 pF → tr ≈ 11 ns.
  — [ADI MT-031 p.6–9](https://www.analog.com/media/en/training-seminars/tutorials/MT-031.pdf)
- ADI ferrite article: for typical 300 kHz–5 MHz switchers, bead+cap LC resonances need damping. Three damping methods are compared on the ADP5071 example:
  - Method A: series 10 Ω resistor in the decoupling-capacitor path. It "degrades the bypass effectiveness at high frequencies".
  - Method B: parallel 10 Ω resistor across the bead.
  - Method C: C_DAMP 1 µF + R_DAMP 2 Ω.
  — [Analog Dialogue 50-02 p.4–5](https://www.analog.com/media/en/analog-dialogue/volume-50/number-1/articles/ferrite-beads-demystified.pdf); [PSD republication](https://www.powersystemsdesign.com/articles/ferrite-bead-demystified/31/10031)

#### RF / module integration (Espressif, u-blox, Nordic)
- Espressif ESP32:
  - Four-layer stack L1 signal/components, L2 complete GND (no signals), L3 power plus a few signals with GND under RF and crystal, L4 few signals and no components.
  - "The width of the main power traces should be no less than 25 mil. The width of VDD3P3 power traces should be no less than 20 mil"; other power traces 12–15 mil.
  - "at least two vias if the main power traces need to cross layers".
  - Chip ground pad "connected to the ground plane through at least nine ground vias".
  - CLC matching in 0201 "close to the pin in a zigzag".
  - A 2nd-harmonic stub on the shunt cap ground pad, "15 mil, … 100 Ω ± 10%" (not needed for 0402 and above).
  - RF trace "routed on the outer layer without vias", consistent width, no branches; "keep all layers clear under the IPEX antenna connector".
  - 2-layer RF trace "normally over 20 mil".
  - SDIO data lines within "± 50 mil" of CLK, 50 Ω ±10%, ideally ≤2000 mil.
  - Touch traces ≤300 mm, width ≤0.18 mm, trace-to-ground gap 0.5–1 mm, electrode diameter 8–15 mm.
  - Supply ripple "<80 mV" (MCS7@11n) and "<120 mV" (11 MHz@11b).
  — [Espressif PCB Layout](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32/pcb-layout-design.html)
- u-blox NORA-B2:
  - Ground plane "minimum size of 24x30 mm, but over 50x50 mm are not recommended" (embedded-antenna variant).
  - Metal parts ≥10 mm from the antenna.
  - Casing clearance ≥10 mm (another bullet says "minimum clearance of 5 mm"; the document is internally inconsistent).
  - "As a first priority, minimize the length of the RF traces. Then, minimize bus length."
  - "All traces, including low speed or DC traces, must couple with a reference plane".
  - "Route the power traces through both the bypass capacitor and bulk capacitor before connecting to the module pin".
  - A stitched ground ring at the PCB edge, with power planes kept back from the edge.
  — [NORA-B2 SIM p.17–20](https://content.u-blox.com/sites/default/files/documents/NORA-B2-u-connectXpress_SIM_UBXDOC-465451970-3906.pdf)
- u-blox MAX-M5Q: cover the area under the receiver "entirely with solder mask. Vias should not be open." — [MAX-M5Q HIM p.18](https://content.u-blox.com/sites/default/files/MAX-M5Q_HardwareIntegrationManual_(UBX-13002460).pdf)
- Nordic nRF52832:
  - "A PCB with a minimum of two layers, including a ground plane, is recommended".
  - "For a PCB with a topside RF ground plane, the VSS pins should be connected directly to the ground plane."
  - Supply for the chip "filtered and routed separately from the supply voltages of any digital circuitry".
  - "Fast switching digital signals should not be routed close to the crystal or the power supply lines."
  - Reference layout: "No components in bottom layer".
  — [nRF52832 PS v1.0 p.542–543](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Wireless/Bluetooth/nRF52832_PS_v1.0.pdf)
- ST AN2867: Cs (stray) = pin capacitance + PCB parasitic, which enters the load-capacitance calculation. Oscillator gain margin must exceed 5. PC13 toggling can shift LSE frequency, so keep PC13 activity away from the LSE. — [ST AN2867 Rev 19 p.11, p.13–14, p.51](https://ecsxtal.com/store/pdf/stm32-microcontroller-application-note.pdf)

### Inferences
- The SPRAAR7 keep-outs (30/50 mil, 90 mil from the plane edge, 1.5 W from voids, anti-pad 30 mil, no non-functional pads) are directly encodable:
  - Pair-to-other-net spacing is native KiCad `clearance` between net classes.
  - Anti-pad size and no-NFP are KiCad pad-stack and zone-clearance settings.
  - "90 mil from the reference-plane edge" and "1.5 W from voids" need the zone-fill-aware geometric checker the high-speed catalog already proposes (G-type). Both are distances to *copper-zone boundaries on an adjacent layer*, not to Edge.Cuts.
- The LearnEMC 2 cm I/O zone gives the placer a concrete default for the "I/O-edge buffer" in the high-speed catalog's EMI posture score. Johnson's 1/6 tr gives a principled "flag for simulation" threshold, with MT-097's tr/2 as a "must terminate" upper bound.
- MT-031's "300 mV between ground planes" and Ott's "no traces cross isolating slots" are checks that apply **only when** a split or isolated region is deliberately present, for example a datasheet-mandated PMIC ground cut as in DA9080.

### Gaps
- Espressif and u-blox give module- or chip-specific numbers (ESP32, NORA-B2, MAX-M5Q). They should be stored per part, not generalised.
- The SPRAAR7 bend-figure annotations (">1.5 x Trace Width", ">5 x Trace Width") are not explained in the text; their exact meaning (segment length? spacing?) was not determinable.

## 3. Implications: which rules are safe as hard constraints vs soft or context-dependent objectives

### Takeaway
Hard constraints are safe for:
1. Fabrication and assembly limits (JLCPCB, courtyards, IPC-2221B HV minimums).
2. **Topological** rules that every primary source states the same way. Examples: no split or void crossing; connector→TVS→IC order with no stub and no via before the TVS; CIN-first with no vias in the hot loop; VOS/FB sensed at the COUT pad; one AGND–PGND tie where the datasheet says "mandatory"; shunt cap grounded through a named pin; antenna keep-out polygon from the module datasheet; zero test points or stubs on SerDes.
3. Vendor numbers **applied only within the scope they were published for** (a TI SoC family's skew, via-count and length tables; data-rate-scaled via stubs).

All millimetre "closeness" numbers that come from blogs, all spacing multipliers (2W, 3W, 5W) and all stitching pitches should be soft, scored objectives with provenance. Contested rules should not be emitted as defaults: 20-H, copper under inductors, CIN/COUT ground separation, stripline vs microstrip, tenting thermal vias, guard traces and "smallest cap closest". They need an explicit context switch.

### Cited Findings
- LearnEMC (Hubing): "boards laid out by people trying to adhere to a long list of EMC guidelines are worse than boards laid out by people with no EMC guidance at all"; "Blindly adhering to EMC design guidelines" heads his 2019 list of layout errors. — [Hubing 2019 slide 3](https://r6.ieee.org/scv-emcs/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/022019ToddHubing.pdf); [LearnEMC PCB Layout](https://learnemc.com/pcb-layout)
- LearnEMC labels even its own "good" guidelines as "not necessarily the best advice in every situation". — [LearnEMC Good Guidelines](https://learnemc.com/other-good-emc-design-guidelines)
- TI makes the TPS62130 AGND/PGND/EP connection "mandatory", while most other statements are "should" or "as close as possible". — [TPS62130 p.28](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps62130.pdf)
- Montrose et al. make the 20-H rule conditional ("recommended only for high-density multilayer assemblies"), and Shim & Hubing measured slightly higher radiation. — [Montrose 2005](https://courseware.zcu.cz/CoursewarePortlets2/DownloadDokumentu?id=186283); [ScholarsMine](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/ele_comeng_facwork/1333)
- Bogatin & Simonovich: guard traces need 3D simulation to quantify and are counter-productive in microstrip. — [p.33](https://www.signalintegrityjournal.com/ext/resources/article-images-2019/Guard-Traces---Love-Them-or-Leave-Them/4567Guard_Trace_DesignCon-2013-rev14_Final.pdf)
- The TI SPRAAR7 tables differ by SoC family: PCIe vias 0 vs 2; PCIe impedance 100 Ω ±10% vs 95 Ω ±5%; inter-pair PCIe skew 550 mil vs 6 ns/1.5 ns. — [TI SPRAAR7J p.16–19](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spraar7)

### Inferences
- **Severity recalibration suggestions:**
  - TL-5 (test points on SerDes) should be P0 or hard ("0 test points").
  - EMI-7's millimetre distance should drop to soft, while its topology stays P0.
  - EMI-6's "10–15 mm" stitch pitch is soft; the P0 is "no crossing a split without a bridge or stitch".
  - PWR-08 should be removed as a default.
  - RF-05 should be split: a hard PCB keep-out from the module figure, plus an enclosure clearance (15 mm for ESP32) exported to the mechanical checklist.
  - AMS-02 can stay S1, but the "unless the datasheet says otherwise" escape conflicts with ADI's own guidance. Keep the escape only for multi-converter parts whose datasheets specify a scheme (the catalog's AD5522 example).
- **Provenance schema refinement:** add `source_scope` (device family / data rate / power level / enclosure vs PCB), `statement_strength` ("mandatory" / "must" / "should" / "as close as possible" / "good guideline"), and `contested_by` (list of rule IDs plus sources). The LLM can then explain why a constraint is hard or soft, and surface disagreements instead of silently choosing.
- **Where an LLM adds value:** turning relative primary-source language ("place the protected IC much further from the TVS than the TVS is to the connector"; "inductor close, but not as close as CIN") into **comparative constraints** (distance ratios or rank orders). These are more faithful to the sources than invented millimetre thresholds, and they remain machine-checkable.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies acceptable hot-loop area in mm² or nH as a pass/fail criterion. EPC's 0.4 nH vs 1.6 nH (30% vs 100% overshoot) and AN-139's 13 nH vs 187 nH are performance data, not limits. The `max_mm2: 15` default in the power catalog's YAML example remains unsourced.
- No primary source gives a numeric RF-to-SMPS or crystal-to-aggressor separation beyond Espressif's 2.7 mm crystal-to-clock-pin gap. These must stay objectives or come from the specific part's datasheet.
- Whether TI's "no stripline" advice in SPRAAR7 is driven by via stubs, loss or fab variation is not explained in the document. The context switch therefore cannot yet be parameterised from the source alone.
