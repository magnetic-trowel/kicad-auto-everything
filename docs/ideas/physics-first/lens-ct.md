# Physics-first ideas: conservation, continuity and topology lens (CT)

Round-2 lens. These ideas follow from one reading of the physics: Kirchhoff's laws are the exact, metric-free half of Maxwell's equations, and a board is a cell complex with cycles, cuts and holes. The argument, the principles CT-P1 to CT-P7 and the computed checks are in [lens-ct](../../topics/physics/lens-ct.md); the script is [ct_cut_topology.py](../../../tools/research/physics/ct_cut_topology.py). The ideas build on [language](language.md) (LA1, LA12, LA20, LA34), [critics](critics.md), [cross-domain](cross-domain.md) (XD2, XD3, XD51, XD70, XD73) and [joint-place-route](joint-place-route.md) (JPR2–JPR4), and do not repeat them.

Tags: *new*, *combo* and *sharp* as in the [catalog](README.md). Effort: S (days), M (weeks), L (months). Everything is untested [I] unless a number is tagged [C].

## A. Cuts, holes and sheets

**CT1. One cut-flux price for every cut.** — *sharp, S.* Price any line of removed conductor (slit, merged antipads, keep-out edge, routing channel through a pour, seam in a shield can, cut in a heat spreader) as ΔE = κ·Φ²/σ, where Φ is the flux the cut intercepts (current, return current, induced shielding current or heat) and σ the sheet conductance. κ = π/4 for an interior cut, π/2 for a cut that touches an insulating boundary (exact, by mirror symmetry), times F(x) = (8/π²)·ln sec(πx/2)/x² when the cut takes a fraction x of the local strip, and ∞ for a cut that closes on itself or severs a terminal. *Principle:* CT-P5, CT-P7. *Beats:* XD2, TM5 and FE2 each price one physics with an empirical edge factor; this is one law with exact constants. [C strip]: zero-width cuts match both ln sec forms within 0.15% for x = 0.1–0.9, and the notch/slit ratio is 2.000 at every x. *Test:* openEMS leakage of a slotted can against Δψ² of the induced current.

**CT2. Crack-augmented adjoint price map.** — *sharp, S.* Add a crack term to the adjoint price map (SIM24, OP38): from the same base solve, read the normal current density J_n along each candidate cut line, expand it in Chebyshev U polynomials, and charge ΔP = πa²R_s·Σc_k²/k times the ligament factor F. Use an exact Woodbury update of the base factorization (rank k = number of cut edges) only for finalists; it is the k-edge form of lens CD's attribution ÷ (1 − leverage) ([lens-cd](../../topics/physics/lens-cd.md)). *Principle:* CT-P7: a cut is a topology change whose cost does not vanish with its width, so an area-based derivative cannot see it. *Beats:* the first-order adjoint underestimates a cut by about its aspect ratio. [C pour]: 29× on the solver-ladder slot, where the crack form is within 9%; on 36 random cuts the adjoint's median ratio is 0.021 and the crack form's 0.95 (75% within ±25%, all within 2×). Lens SY's (π/4)·R_s·Δψ_u·Δψ_λ extends the crack term to any objective through the adjoint's stream function ([lens-sy](../../topics/physics/lens-sy.md)). *Test:* 1,000 cuts on 10 real pours, predicted against the exact cost.

**CT3. Euler-characteristic event log for references.** — *sharp, S.* Track b0 (islands) and b1 (holes) of every declared reference layer across router and optimizer iterations. Any change is logged with its location and priced at once: a new hole by CT1 against the critical nets above it, a new island by its quarter-wave and its connection. *Principle:* CT-P5. *Beats:* PC8 finds plane perforation after routing; this stops it inside the loop for the cost of counting polygon rings. *Test:* Freerouting runs on the qualified suite: events against post-hoc PLANE-PERF findings.

**CT4. Persistence margin of reference webs.** — *combo, S.* For each antipad cluster under a critical net, compute the clearance growth δ* at which neighbouring holes merge into one slot (half the narrowest web), and report it as a topological robustness margin against etch and registration (TM31); price the merged slot with CT1. *Principle:* CT-P5. *Beats:* FE4 and XD95 find narrow webs; this states how much process variation turns a harmless sieve into a slot. *Test:* σ-margin of 20 BGA breakouts; slot appearance in erode-dilate sweeps.

**CT5. Seams and apertures along the shielding-current streamlines.** — *combo, M.* For shield cans, enclosure seams and plane slots near cables, solve the induced surface current at the band of concern once and orient seams and slots along its ψ contours. Aperture leakage then follows CT1, which is why the longest dimension, not the area, sets leakage. *Principle:* CT-P7 transferred from DC pours to shields. *Beats:* the λ/20 aperture slogan ignores orientation. *Test:* openEMS can with a slot at 0°, 45° and 90° to the induced current.

**CT6. Shorted-turn critic.** — *new, S.* Enumerate the H1 generators of each net's copper: holes in pours, closed rings, and loops closed through vias across layers. Flag those that link the flux of a magnetic part (inductor or transformer axis, stray dipole) or of a hot loop, and report the EMF jωΦ and the circulating current. The fix is a radial slit that breaks the ring. *Principle:* CT-P5 (each hole is one circulation mode, driven by the flux through it). *Beats:* no round-1 critic sees closed rings; magnetics practice has always slit shields and bands. *Test:* planar-transformer and buck boards with pours around the inductor; PEEC circulating current with and without a slit.

**CT7. Notch, don't hole, under open-core magnetics.** — *sharp, S.* When copper under an open drum core must go, cut a notch to the pour edge rather than a closed cut-out. A hole conserves AC flux through itself (its rim is a shorted turn above its L/R corner); a notch has no rim. *Principle:* CT-P5. *Beats:* the Rohm/TI dispute (P13, PW46) compares only "solid" against "removed". *Test:* PEEC of a plane with a hole, a notch and no cut under a drum core: L, loss and rim current.

**CT8. Hole-mode augmented PEEC for perforated planes.** — *new, M.* Model a plane with holes as an image plane plus one circulation unknown per hole (its harmonic mode), instead of meshing the plane. The hole modes carry the slot detours and shorted turns that an image plane misses, at F1.5 cost. *Principle:* CT-P5 (Hodge decomposition: image part plus b1 harmonic modes). *Beats:* SIM16 and SIM17 either ignore holes or mesh them. *Test:* against meshed PEEC ([fe_peec_plane.py](../../../tools/research/physics/fe_peec_plane.py)) for planes with 1–20 holes.

## B. The board–world cut and external cycles

**CT9. Board–world charge ledger.** — *new, M.* For a board with one cable, the common-mode current is the time derivative of the board's external charge: I_CM = −jω·Σ_i C_i,ext·V_i. One electrostatic solve, with the outside at 1 V and every board conductor at 0 V, gives C_i,ext for all conductors at once (reciprocity). Multiply by the node spectra from schematic simulation (DR1) to get one ledger row per band, with every conductor's share and phase. *Principle:* CT-P1 on the board's boundary surface. *Beats:* FE17 and PC22 treat parts one at a time and ignore phase. *Test:* openEMS board with a 1 m cable on 5 layouts: I_CM against the ledger.

**CT10. Antiphase charge balancing.** — *new, M.* Make Σ C_i,ext·dV_i/dt small by pairing complementary dv/dt nodes (bridge legs, transformer winding ends, push-pull drains) with matched external capacitance: mirrored switch-node copper, a symmetric heatsink, matched tab exposure. The objective term is |Σ C_i,ext·V_i(f)|² per band. *Principle:* CT-P1. *Beats:* "minimize SW area" treats each node alone; a 10% mismatch already leaves only one tenth of the single-ended current [I]. *Test:* full bridge, mirrored vs unmirrored SW copper, simulated CM current.

**CT11. Close-locally operator with a current-division target.** — *combo, S.* For every displacement edge from a board node to an external body, add an internal closing edge (strap to the source node, Y-capacitor, shield layer) and size it so the external share Z_local/(Z_local + Z_ext) meets the budget in each band. [C cm]: a 10 nH heatsink strap to DC− passes 1.3% of the 22 pF displacement current to the LISN at 30 MHz (−39 dB against a chassis bond) but 7% at 100 MHz; adding a 4.7 nF Y-capacitor brings 30 MHz to 0.02%. *Principle:* CT-P1, CT-P4. *Beats:* DR28 and FE14 choose a bond node but do not size the local cycle or say where it stops working. *Test:* motor-drive bench with the four bond options.

**CT12. Extended-graph cycle census per band.** — *new, M.* Build the extended graph: board conductors from the board physics model (PC1), parasitic capacitances above a threshold, chassis, heatsinks, cable shields and conductors, LISN or earth. Compute a minimum cycle basis per band, label each cycle internal or external and driven or undriven, and rank cycles by current from one nodal solve. Ground loops and common-mode paths then have names. *Principle:* CT-P4. *Beats:* LA12 finds loops in the schematic only; the dangerous loops close through the realization and the world. A minimum-|Z| spanning tree makes each chord's fundamental cycle its least-impedance closure: in the script's half-bridge, bonding the heatsink to DC− moves the driven cycle from external (through the LISN) to internal [C cm]. *Test:* recover the documented CM path in 10 EMC failure write-ups.

**CT13. Frequency persistence of the reference topology.** — *new, S.* For every tie between reference regions (net tie, capacitor, ferrite, single via), compute the frequencies at which parasitic edges change the cycle structure: a capacitive bridge of C_par across a tie of inductance L_tie becomes the dominant path above f = 1/(2π·√(L_tie·C_par)); a ferrite tie disappears above its crossover. Report a barcode per pair of regions: a 1 nH tie under an island with 76 pF of overlap to the next plane stops being a single point near 580 MHz [I]. *Principle:* CT-P4, CT-P6. *Beats:* LA20 and LA34 assume their band assignments hold; this says where they do. *Test:* SPICE of 5 split-ground boards with plane-overlap capacitance.

**CT14. Ground-loop resonance list.** — *new, S.* Every cycle created by a parasitic capacitance in series with an inductive tie is a series LC loop. List them (split-plane tie plus overlap C, heatsink strap plus tab C, shield pigtail plus cable C) with their resonances, and add them to the frequency plan (S8, FE13). *Principle:* CT-P4. *Beats:* the frequency plan covers cavities, slots and ring frequencies, not ground-loop resonances. *Test:* SPICE and openEMS of a split plane with one tie.

**CT15. Closure type for every port.** — *sharp, S.* Replace LA1's yes/no "has a named return" with a closure type per port and band: conduction (a Loop, budget in nH), displacement inside the board (a Node, budget in pF to a named neighbour), or displacement to the world (an Antenna, budget in µA of CM). FastHenry's NaN for a port without a conductive return is a property of magnetoquasistatics, not of physics. *Principle:* CT-P1. *Beats:* a binary half-port check calls an antenna a typing error. *Test:* classify 200 ports on 10 boards; every CM finding should come from the third class.

**CT16. World node mandatory in back-annotation.** — *sharp, S.* The physical netlist used for the layout invisibility index must contain a world node, the chassis, heatsinks and cable conductors, each joined by its C_ext from CT9. Without external edges, KCL on the board's boundary forces zero CM current, so the index is blind to emissions. *Principle:* CT-P1. *Beats:* DR7 splits ground into many nodes but keeps the board closed. *Test:* LII with and without the world node on the 48 V half-bridge case of the worked thought experiments.

**CT17. Test setups as relative cycles.** — *combo, M.* Add each compliance test's injection and return to the extended graph (ESD gun and strap, EFT clamp, BCI clamp, LISN and ground plane). For each, compute the path of least impedance from injection to return through the board, and list the board conductors it shares with sensitive cycles. *Principle:* CT-P4 with relative cycles. *Beats:* DR47 does this for ESD only; LearnEMC asks for "each test's current path" by hand. *Test:* predicted shared conductors against failure sites in 10 immunity reports.

## C. Exclusion, necks and grounding topology

**CT18. Block–cut exclusion certificate.** — *sharp, S.* Run Tarjan's biconnected-components algorithm on the copper conduction graph. A victim region whose block lies off every aggressor's source–sink path in the block–cut tree carries exactly zero DC aggressor current, with no solve. Fall back to the DC solve (LA7, XD72) only where no certificate exists. *Principle:* CT-P6. *Beats:* exclusion checks need a field solve per aggressor; certificates are exact and linear-time. *Test:* certificates against DC solves on 20 mixed-signal boards.

**CT19. Put the neck at the aggressor's stagnation point.** — *new, S.* A peninsula's neck (the star point) should sit where the aggressor's tangential field along the isolating slot is zero: a stagnation point or an equipotential. The leakage then falls from about w² to about w⁴ in neck width w. [C neck]: for a 1.6 mm neck in a 0.4 mm slot ring, the flank position leaves 5.6×10⁻³ of the solid-plane gradient in the island and the stagnation position 4.3×10⁻⁶, 1,300× less. *Principle:* CT-P6 with the aperture expansion. *Beats:* DR26 optimizes neck width only, and finds that a 4 mm flank neck still leaks. *Test:* 2-D solves on 10 ADC layouts; measured offset on one board with a movable load.

**CT20. Cycle-space grounding synthesis per band.** — *new, M.* Make the reference topology a design variable. Choose, per pair of regions, which ties exist (net tie, capacitor, ferrite, open) so that each band's cycle space is a tree below f_x and a mesh above, subject to safety bonds. Solve as a small MILP over tie types and positions, with CT18 certificates and CT13 crossovers as constraints. *Principle:* CT-P4, CT-P6. *Beats:* RG19 and LA34 state the rule; this synthesizes the ties. *Test:* re-design 5 mixed-signal grounds; simulated noise against a single plane and a hard split.

**CT21. Homological return pairing.** — *new, M.* Answer the open question of which pin returns which signal. In each band, a signal's return is the cycle of least impedance through the signal's edge in the extended graph; the pins where that cycle enters each part are its return pins, and the next-best cycles are the alternatives. *Principle:* CT-P4. *Beats:* LA1 pairs returns from IBIS or naming, without checking that the copper realizes them. *Test:* agreement with pin-role pairing on 20 boards; every disagreement is a finding.

## D. Coupling and closure

**CT22. Cycle-honest coupling ledger.** — *sharp, S.* Define every coupling budget on a closed victim cycle. The validator refuses an AC "voltage between two points" unless the victim's closing conductor is named. Compute M(aggressor cycle, victim cycle), which is gauge-invariant, and use partial elements only to attribute it. [C closure]: a victim whose own ground via sits 0.5–2 mm from a TVS ground via sees 47–82% of that via's partial inductance; a victim whose reference runs 0.5–5 mm beside a shared ground trace sees 30–73%. *Principle:* CT-P2. *Beats:* LA6, LA21 and DR47 charge the shared segment's partial L, which is only a bound here, and not a bound in general. *Test:* PEEC victim-cycle EMF against shared-L_p rows on 20 shared-return cases.

**CT23. Victim closure as a design lever.** — *combo, S.* Route the victim's reference conductor through the same flux tube as the aggressor's shared segment (a ground via next to the TVS via, a return beside the shared trace), so it sees L_p − M and "bounces together" with the aggressor. *Principle:* CT-P2. *Beats:* the usual advice only separates paths; sometimes co-locating the closure is cheaper. *Test:* TLP or ESD-gun measurements on two layouts with the same parts.

**CT24. Plane-aware nesting pre-screen.** — *sharp, S.* For each aggressor–victim loop pair: a shared edge means conductive coupling (exact flag); a victim nested inside the aggressor means strong coupling when no plane is near (free space: 1,060 pH nested and centred against −32 pH at an 8.5 mm gap [C nest]); otherwise coupling decays metrically. Over a close plane every conductor closes through its own image, so nesting fixes only the sign of M and the magnitude follows the nearest conductor distance (2.5 pH nested and centred, 45 pH at 0.5 mm inside, −43 pH at 0.5 mm outside [C nest]). *Principle:* CT-P2 (which surface carries the flux). *Beats:* P17 ("no loop inside another") is right on two-layer boards and misleading over planes. *Test:* rank correlation with PEEC M on 2-layer and 4-layer boards.

**CT25. Filter-cut integrity.** — *new, S.* A filter is a cut vertex of the conduction graph between a noisy and a clean side. Extract the displacement edges across the same cut (capacitance between input and output traces, pours and parts) and report the filter's isolation ceiling 1/(ωC_bypass·Z). Hubing's "bypassing filters" becomes a number. *Principle:* CT-P4. *Beats:* FILTER-POS checks the order of parts on the path, not the bypass. *Test:* 2-D capacitance extraction and SPICE on 10 I/O filters.

**CT26. KCL and KVL violation numbers as regime gates.** — *new, S.* Per node, K_C = ω·C_par·|Z_node|; per loop, K_L = ω·L_par/|Z_loop|. Where K_C or K_L exceeds the budget-relative threshold, the schematic stops being true and the structure gets a C or an L budget. Examples: a 1 mΩ shunt with 1 nH reaches K_L = 1 at 160 kHz; a 100 kΩ feedback node with 0.2 pF reaches K_C = 1 at 8 MHz. *Principle:* CT-P1, CT-P2. *Beats:* LA4 types nodes and loops by role; these numbers type them by physics. *Test:* agreement with expert criticality maps on 10 boards.

## E. Joint state, critics and verification

**CT27. Topological gate first, metric weight second.** — *combo, S.* Every critic first evaluates an exact discrete predicate (a Betti number, cut membership, cycle overlap, a winding or return word) and computes a metric magnitude only where the gate fires. 13 of the 39 physics critics already have such a core (RET-COV, RET-HOP, RET-VIA, RET-REFNET, PLANE-PERF, SPLIT-X, GND-ISLAND, TOPO, PWR-TRACE, KELVIN, FILTER-POS, ANT-KEEP, VIA-STUB). *Principle:* CT-P7. *Beats:* proxy thresholds give false negatives near cliffs; gates are exact and cost milliseconds. *Test:* gates alone on the mutation benchmark (PC29): recall and cost against the full critics.

**CT28. Physics topological events in the joint state.** — *new, M.* Extend JPR2/JPR3 certificates with physics events: a shadow entering a reference hole, a change of b0 or b1 of a reference, a loop becoming nested, an articulation point appearing or vanishing, a new cycle in the reference network, a closed ring around a magnetic part. Use adjoint gradients between events and charge event costs (CT1, CT18, CT6) at them. *Principle:* CT-P7. *Beats:* the chained-adjoint synergy (S1) is silently wrong across events. *Test:* joint SA with and without event pricing; count accepted moves later rejected by critics.

**CT29. Return word per critical net.** — *new, S.* Store, beside each critical net's routing homotopy class, the word of reference holes its shadow crosses: empty means a continuous return; each letter is a priced crossing (CT1, FE2). The word updates incrementally as parts move and explains itself ("USB_DP crosses slot S3 and antipad chain A7"). *Principle:* CT-P5. *Beats:* RET-COV recomputes geometry and returns a percentage. *Test:* words against RET-COV findings on the qualified suite.

**CT30. Topological invisibility test.** — *new, M.* In each band, contract parasitic edges whose impedance is far below the circuit's and delete those far above. The layout is structurally invisible if the result equals the schematic's band graph. Any extra cycle or merged node is a named structural parasitic: a ground loop, a CM path, a sneak path. *Principle:* CT-P4. *Beats:* the layout invisibility index measures magnitude, not structure. *Test:* planted structural faults (extra ground loop, heatsink to chassis) detected; benign twins quiet.

**CT31. Cells as minimum HF cuts.** — *combo, M.* Define DR22's cells by a normalized minimum cut of the HF current graph (edge weight = band current or coupling), so each cell boundary crosses the least HF current. The locality index (DR42) becomes Σ|I| across the cut over Σ|I| inside. *Principle:* CT-P1 on cell boundaries. *Beats:* template-defined cells do not follow the copper actually drawn. *Test:* cut-defined cells against expert partitions and an EMI proxy on 10 boards.

**CT32. Ampère-loop observables in full-wave runs.** — *new, S.* In openEMS or Palace runs, measure the net current through declared cuts (cable bundles, cell boundaries, zone boundaries) as ∮H·dl on probe loops around them. These are mesh-robust, metric-free observables for calibration (CM current, cell escape). *Principle:* CT-P3. *Beats:* port-voltage-based CM estimates depend on where the reference is taken. *Test:* agreement with port-current CM on calibration coupons.

## Most promising

1. **CT2** (crack-augmented adjoint): restores "one solve prices every edit" for cuts, with a computed check.
2. **CT9 + CT11** (board–world ledger, close locally): turns the one-cable CM problem into one electrostatic solve and a sized local cycle.
3. **CT27 + CT28** (topological gates and events): an exact, cheap layer under every critic and the trust region for every gradient.
4. **CT19** (neck at the stagnation point): a placement rule worth three orders of magnitude, from a 2-D solve.
5. **CT22** (cycle-honest ledger): fixes a bookkeeping error before it enters the IR.
