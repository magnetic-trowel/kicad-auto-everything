# Cross-domain ideas for physics-first layout (XD)

Workstream 10. These are ideas from fields that already shape conductors, fields, flows, heat and structures from physics: coil and magnet design, power modules, IC power grids, inverse design, flow networks, structures, harness and audio practice, quantum chips, biology, graphics and robotics, grid engineering and planning. The earlier transplant inventories ([cross-domain-transplants](../cross-domain-transplants.md), [routing transplants](../../topics/routing/cross-domain-routing-transplants.md), [packing transplants](../../topics/placement/packing-and-graph-drawing-transplants.md), [scene transplants](../../topics/placement/scene-layout-transplants.md)) cover search algorithms and LLM workflows, and none of that is repeated here. The analogies with the biggest payoff are worked through in [cross-domain-lessons](../../topics/physics/cross-domain-lessons.md).

**Format.** Each entry gives an id, a name, tags, the mechanism, the physics, why it beats the rule of thumb, and a test.
- Tags: **new** (we found no PCB use), **combo** (a new combination of known parts), **sharp** (a known idea made buildable or quantitative).
- Effort: S (days), M (weeks), L (months).
- Evidence: [P] read in the primary source, [S] snippet or secondary, [V] vendor claim, [I] our inference, [C] computed by us.

**Scripts.** All [C] numbers come from four scripts in `tools/research/physics/`, each deterministic and under a minute:
- [xd_sheet.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_sheet.py): finite-difference copper sheets: corners, pours, slots, via fields.
- [xd_power_tree.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_power_tree.py): width exponents, star vs trunk, reverse return.
- [xd_lengths.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_lengths.py): thermal and PDN screening lengths, paralleled layers, I²t, step voltage.
- [xd_loop_coupling.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_loop_coupling.py): Neumann mutual inductance with plane images.

**Defaults.** Unless stated otherwise: 1 oz copper (35 µm, R_s = 0.49 mΩ/sq) and DC.

## A. Coil design by stream functions (MRI gradient coils, stellarator coils, magnetometry)

The shared mechanism: a divergence-free sheet current J on a surface is written as J = ∇ψ × n. Contours of ψ are current streamlines, and the current between two points equals ψ(b) − ψ(a). MRI and fusion coil design optimize ψ, then turn its contours into conductors.

- **XD1 Stream-function pour designer** (new, M). Mesh the allowed region of a copper layer as triangles. Solve for ψ to minimize resistive loss plus a frequency-weighted magnetic energy, subject to terminal currents and keep-outs, using bfieldtools' mesh operators (BSD-3) [P]. Then make copper where |∇ψ| is above a threshold, with outlines that follow ψ contours. A hand-drawn pour fixes the shape first and lets Laplace pick the current; this chooses the current first and derives the shape from it. Test: on 5 converter boards, compare DC loss and PEEC loop inductance with the existing pours at equal copper area.
- **XD2 Slot damage = Δψ² check** (new, S). Compute ψ once for the operating current. The extra resistance from any proposed slit, antipad chain or keep-out is then ΔR ≈ (π/4)·R_s·(Δψ/I)², where Δψ is the stream-function difference between the slit's ends. An edge notch costs about twice that. One solve then scores thousands of candidate cuts in O(1) each. [C] on a 20 mm strip, this predicts the FD result to within 12–19% (low) for centred slits of 2, 5 and 10 mm (0.008/0.049/0.196 sq predicted vs 0.009/0.055/0.233 sq). The same law holds for plane-pair spreading inductance below cavity resonance, with R_s replaced by jωμ0·d. It applies to spread-out sheet current. A narrow return stream under a trace that crosses a slot is a different regime (Δψ = I, and the cost grows roughly as ℓ·ln ℓ; see [physics-critics-validation](../../topics/physics/physics-critics-validation.md)). Test: 100 random cuts on real pours, predicted vs FD ΔR; then use the law as the router's cost for antipad chains.
- **XD3 Cut along streamlines** (sharp, S). When a plane must be cut or perforated (via rows, isolation gaps, connector cut-outs), orient the cut along ψ contours of the dominant currents. [C] a 10 mm slit along the flow costs 0.003 sq; across the flow it costs 0.233 sq, about 90× more. This replaces "never slot a plane" with "never slot across the current that matters, at the frequency that matters". Test: the XD2 harness, comparing orientation choices.
- **XD4 Contours as conductors** (sharp, M). For planar coils (wireless power, inductive sensors, field-nulling and calibration coils), lay traces along equally spaced ψ contours. Then solve the "connect the loops across layers" step, which a 2025 OPM-MEG pipeline did for 1.5 m biplanar PCB coils [S]. Test: reproduce a published PCB coil and compare field error with a hand-wound spiral.
- **XD5 REGCOIL regularization knob** (sharp, S–M). Stellarator coil design adds a Tikhonov term on current density and tunes it to reach a target peak current density, which sets coil spacing (Landreman 2017) [S]. For copper, sweeping that term traces a Pareto front of physics error against "copper complexity" (peak J, number of features). The front is a single curve the LLM can pick a point from. Test: sweep on one hot loop and plot loop inductance against feature count.
- **XD6 Minimum-inductance return design** (combo, M). Turner's minimum-inductance coils (1988) [S] solve for the current that stores the least magnetic energy. With the forward conductor fixed, solve for the return-sheet current in the allowed region of the adjacent layer. The solution shows where return copper must stay continuous and where voids are free. Test: compare predicted "free void" regions with PEEC loop L after voiding them.
- **XD7 Active shielding of hot loops** (new, M). Mansfield–Chapman shielded gradient coils (1986) [S] add a second winding that cancels the external field. On 2-layer boards without a full plane, design the bottom-layer return copper so the hot loop's net dipole moment is near zero. Test: Biot–Savart far field at 3 m, compared with the usual "return under it" layout.
- **XD8 Minimax current density** (sharp, M). L∞ coil design minimizes peak |J| rather than total power (arXiv 1002.3413) [S]. Apply this to pours whose limit is a hot spot (necks, corners at pad entries) rather than total loss. It tells you where fillets and widening pay. Test: peak |J| and thermal hot spot against loss-optimal copper.
- **XD9 Coil-curve penalties for critical traces** (new, M). FOCUS and SIMSOPT (MIT licence from v1.0 [S]) represent coils as Fourier curves. They optimize a physics objective (differentiable Biot–Savart) plus curvature, length and coil-to-coil distance penalties. For PCB traces those penalties map to minimum bend radius, length and clearance, and the physics term becomes field coupling to a victim or a magnetometer. Test: re-shape a sense trace near an inductor and measure M before and after.
- **XD10 Winding surface ↔ stackup height** (combo, M). Stellarator work optimizes the winding surface by adjoint (Paul et al. 2018) [S]. Here the "surface" is the layer and dielectric height of each copper sheet. Report the sensitivity of each physics budget to each dielectric thickness so the stackup is chosen with the layout, not before it. Test: finite-difference check of the adjoint sensitivities on 3 stackups.
- **XD11 Figure-8 and mirrored loops** (sharp, S). RFIC figure-8 inductors cancel far fields [S]. Symmetric input capacitors on both sides of a converter IC do the same: TI reports about 4 dB lower FM-band noise for LMR38020 [V]. [C] two opposed 3×3 mm loops in place of one 3×6 mm loop give exact cancellation on the symmetry plane, and −7/−13/−19 dB at 10/20/40 mm along the pair axis. A 60/40 current imbalance leaves −20 dB. Make "split the loop into mirrored halves" a placement option for any hot loop, and budget the imbalance explicitly. Test: PEEC and near-field scan of a dual-cap buck vs a single-cap buck.

## B. Power modules, busbars, planar magnetics

- **XD12 Commutation cells as the placement unit** (sharp, S). The P-cell/N-cell decomposition (Li, Tolbert, Wang, Peng, ECCE 2010) [S] groups the switch, its complementary device and the capacitor that closes the commutation loop. Place cells, not parts; this is what N7 should place. Test: count loops placed as cells on 20 open converter boards and compare with the overshoot data in [hot-loops](../../topics/rules/power/hot-loops.md).
- **XD13 Laminated-busbar primitive** (combo, S–M). A stacked "+ over −" pour pair at dielectric d has L′ ≈ μ0·d/w per unit length, plus a fringing correction. Make it a typed object with width, overlap margin, layer pair and current, so the placer composes busbars rather than drawing traces. [I] for d = 0.2 mm and w = 10 mm, L′ ≈ 25 nH/m (0.25 nH/cm). Test: PEEC against the closed form for 5 aspect ratios.
- **XD14 Equalize paralleled loops, not just minimize them** (sharp, S). Module designers equalize commutation and common-source inductance across paralleled dies, because unequal L means unequal switching current. Make the objective for paralleled FETs the spread of per-device loop L and gate-loop L. Test: PEEC on a 4-FET bank, current spread in SPICE.
- **XD15 Gate loop orthogonal to power loop** (sharp, S). Compute M between the gate-drive loop and the power loop (Neumann) and budget it (e.g., |M| < 0.1 nH) instead of writing "keep the gate loop small". Perpendicular planes, or a placement on the power loop's magnetic null line, makes M ≈ 0. Test: [xd_loop_coupling.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_loop_coupling.py)-style M on 10 gate-driver layouts.
- **XD16 PowerSynth as a reference engine** (sharp, M). PowerSynth 2 (GPL-3.0, so compatible) [P] lays out power modules with electrical and thermal models. Mine its fast parasitic models and layout-optimization loop for converter cells. Test: run its models on a PCB half-bridge and compare with PEEC.
- **XD17 Proximity check for paralleled layers** (sharp, S). Paralleling copper layers for one net helps only at DC. Above f ≈ R/(2πL), the AC current crowds into the layer nearest its return. [C] a 4-layer board with the net on L1, L3 and L4 and the return on L2: 33/33/33% at DC, 75% in L1 at 100 kHz, 86% at ≥ 1 MHz. Check ripple current per layer, not just DC width. Test: the lumped model vs a 2-D PEEC cross-section.
- **XD18 Interleaved current layers** (sharp, S). Planar transformers interleave windings to cut Dowell AC resistance (Dowell 1966) [S]. For high-ripple paths, stack + − + − so that each forward layer has its own adjacent return. This lowers both AC resistance and loop inductance. Test: XD17 model with an interleaved stackup.
- **XD19 Damping set by sqrt(L/C)** (sharp, S). Modules damp the loop L with C_oss ringing using an RC snubber of R ≈ sqrt(L_loop/C_oss) placed across the loop. Size it from the extracted L instead of a datasheet default. Test: SPICE with the extracted loop.
- **XD20 Electrothermal topology optimization of busbars → pours** (combo, M). Thermal topology optimization of a three-layer laminated busbar (HAL hal-01735587) [S] is the closest prior art to electrothermal copper TO. Share a benchmark with workstream 6 ([copper-shape-synthesis](../../topics/physics/copper-shape-synthesis.md)).

## C. IC power grids, interconnect and packaging

- **XD21 Signoff-style IR and current-density maps** (sharp, S–M). On-chip grids get IR-drop and electromigration signoff (a sparse solve plus |J| maps). Do the same per rail on the copper: worst-case mV drop at each load pin, and |J| against an ampacity map. Local queries use random-walk solvers (Qian, Nassif, Sapatnekar 2005) [S]. Test: compare with a commercial DC-drop tool on 3 boards.
- **XD22 Via rows across the flow, sized by transfer length** (new for PCB, S). Current moves between two overlapping layers within a transfer length L_T = p·sqrt(R_via/(R_s1 + R_s2)). This is Berger's contact TLM (1972) and Volkersen's shear lag (1938) [S]. [C] through-vias at 1 mm pitch give L_T = 1.05 mm. In a 10×4 via field, the two end columns carry 59% of the current and the middle six carry 16%. Deleting the 32 inner vias raises resistance by only 1.8%. With short blind vias (L_T = 0.37 mm) the end columns carry 86%. Siemens reports the same from HyperLynx: "a handful of vias in one corner" do the work [V]. Replace "N vias per amp" with "vias in rows across the flow at the entry and exit edges, with per-via current from the share". Test: [xd_sheet.py vias](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_sheet.py) on real transitions; thermal-camera check of via heating.
- **XD23 Short-neck immunity (Blech analogy)** (sharp, S). In ICs, segments shorter than the Blech length resist electromigration. On a PCB, a neck shorter than its thermal spreading length dumps heat into the wide copper on both sides, so a short narrow neck runs much cooler than IPC width tables imply. Rate necks by length and width together. Test: FD electrothermal sweep of neck length against peak temperature.
- **XD24 Ball-map logic for connector pinouts** (sharp, S). Packages assign power and ground balls in checkerboards to minimize loop inductance. Treat connector and MCU/FPGA pin assignment as the same problem: each fast signal or current pin gets an adjacent return pin, scored by computed loop L. This extends P9 with a physics objective. Test: score 20 open-hardware connector pinouts.
- **XD25 Shield insertion and net ordering (SINO)** (sharp, M). He and Lepak (ISPD 2000) [S] order bus nets and insert shields to bound inductive and capacitive coupling. Apply the same to PCB bus corridors with the crosstalk kernel 1/(1+(d/h)²) (Johnson). Test: worst-pair coupling against random order on 10 buses.
- **XD26 Etch-aware geometry (OPC analogy)** (sharp, M). Lithography pre-distorts masks. For RF filters, couplers and impedance-critical lines, design for the as-etched trapezoid and bias. Score the layout with the fab's etch factor applied, not the drawn copper. Test: openEMS (GPL-3) of drawn vs as-etched coupled-line filters.
- **XD27 Physics-aware copper thieving (CMP fill analogy)** (sharp, S). IC dummy fill follows density rules. PCB thieving should do the same, plus physics rules: no floating copper near antennas and RF (it detunes and resonates), grounded and stitched fill elsewhere. Test: count floating fill islands in RF zones on open boards.
- **XD28 Passive macromodels per copper region** (sharp, M). Make reduced-order models of each pour or plane region for fast SPICE co-simulation of the PDN and hot loops. PRIMA (Odabasioglu 1998) [S] gives passive reduction; vector fitting (Gustavsen and Semlyen 1999, originally from power systems) [S] fits measured or simulated responses. Test: macromodel against the full PEEC on one buck.
- **XD29 Slack-reweighted nets (timing-driven placement → budget-driven)** (combo, S). IC placers raise net weights by timing slack. Reweight placement nets by remaining physics-budget slack (nH, mV, dB) after each evaluation, building on I1 and I5. Test: budget violations after 5 reweighting rounds vs static weights.
- **XD30 Hierarchical compression of coupling matrices** (sharp, M). IC extraction made FMM and H-matrix compression standard (FastCap and FastHenry lineage). Use it in board PEEC so whole-board loop and plane models stay O(N log N). See [open-solvers](../../topics/physics/open-solvers.md) for licences.

## D. Microwave, antenna and photonic inverse design

- **XD31 Adjoint copper heat maps** (sharp, M). One adjoint solve gives the sensitivity of IR drop, loop inductance or an S-parameter to copper at every cell. Show it as "add copper here, remove copper here" for the LLM and the human. Shared with workstream 6 ([physics-in-the-loop-optimization](../../topics/physics/physics-in-the-loop-optimization.md)).
- **XD32 DRC rules inside the optimizer** (sharp, M). Hammond, Oskooi, Johnson and Ralph (Opt. Express 2021) [S] enforce foundry minimum width, spacing and area with differentiable morphological transforms. Put PCB minimum width, clearance and minimum area inside density optimization, so the result is DRC-clean before polygonization. Test: DRC violations after legalization, with and without.
- **XD33 Robust etch optimization (eroded, nominal, dilated)** (sharp, M). Wang, Lazarov and Sigmund (2011) [S] optimize the worst of three projected designs. For PCBs, use ±etch bias (e.g., ±25 µm) and layer-to-layer registration, so the physics survives fab variation. Test: Monte-Carlo over bias of nominal-optimized vs robust-optimized filters.
- **XD34 Characteristic modes of the ground structure** (new for PCB, M–L). Characteristic mode analysis (Garbacz 1971; Harrington and Mautz 1971) finds the natural radiating current modes of a conductor. Run it on board, cable shield and heatsink, then place noise sources where the dominant modes' weighting coefficients are small. A heatsink study did exactly this with dipole sources, with < 3 dB error against full-wave [S]. Test: MoM on a board plus cable stub; rank 5 source positions and check with openEMS.
- **XD35 Reciprocity susceptibility maps** (sharp, S–M). For each victim (ADC input loop, crystal, antenna feed), solve once for the field a unit current in the victim would produce. By reciprocity, coupling from an aggressor anywhere on the board is then an inner product, so placement queries become lookups. This complements the schematic-level adjoint in [design reasoning](../../topics/physics/design-reasoning-architecture.md). Test: map-predicted M vs Neumann M for 50 aggressor positions.
- **XD36 Bloch analysis for periodic features** (new, M). Via fences, BGA via fields, stitching grids and hatched planes are periodic structures. Their band structure gives stop bands and resonances directly. Choose pitch so a stop band covers the aggressor's harmonics, instead of "λ/20". Test: unit-cell eigen-solve vs a full via-fence simulation.
- **XD37 Coaxial via cages** (sharp, S). Microwave practice surrounds a signal via with return vias on a circle whose radius gives the target coaxial impedance, Z0 ≈ (60/√εr)·ln(D/d). Generate cages from that formula rather than "one ground via nearby". Test: TDR simulation of 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6-via cages.
- **XD38 Level-set copper boundaries** (sharp, M). Level-set topology optimization (structures, photonics) moves smooth boundaries by shape derivatives and changes topology naturally. Represent pour outlines as level sets so optimized copper stays CAD-like. Test: polygon count and smoothness vs SIMP output at equal objective.

## E. Flow networks: Murray, Kelvin, transport networks, plumbing, power grids

- **XD39 Width exponent chosen by objective (electrical Murray's law)** (new, S). At a fixed copper area, w ∝ I (constant current density, Kelvin's law of 1881) minimizes total I²R. Minimizing the worst-case drop needs wider trunks and starved near branches. IPC equal-temperature widths (w ∝ I^1.38) are a thermal limit, not an optimum. [C] on a 5-load comb tree:
  - equal widths: 100% loss, 100% worst drop;
  - w ∝ I: 52% loss, 89% drop;
  - minimax-drop optimum: 76% loss, 60% drop;
  - IPC form: 58% loss, 135% drop.

  Size widths from the declared objective, with IPC as a floor only. Test: re-size the power nets of 10 open boards at equal copper area.
- **XD40 Junction conservation check** (sharp, S). At loss-optimal branching, the parent width equals the sum of the children's widths. That is da Vinci's area-preserving rule; Murray's law is the cube-law version for blood. Flag junctions where the parent is narrower than the sum, which means a bottleneck. Test: count violations on open boards.
- **XD41 Varignon junction relaxation** (new, S–M). Branch points of power trees sit where "tensions" T(I) = c0 + a·I balance. Here c0 is the clearance overhead per conductor and a·I the copper (Gilbert's 1967 flow-dependent Steiner trees; Xia's branched transport). A string-tension physics engine relaxes junctions to the optimum. [C] with zero overhead, a star of separate conductors beats a shared trunk (61 vs 96 mW). Per-conductor overhead above about 0.43 mm flips it (126 vs 96 mW at 0.6 mm). Test: relaxed junctions vs hand-drawn trees on 5 boards.
- **XD42 Tree, mesh or pour by the γ rule** (new, S). Optimal transport networks are trees when cost grows as conductance^γ with γ < 1, and sheets when γ > 1 (Bohn and Magnasco 2007, as summarized by Katifori et al.) [S]. Fixed-thickness copper has conductance ∝ width ∝ cost, so γ = 1, exactly at the transition. Clearance overhead tips it toward trees; fluctuating loads and failure tolerance tip it toward loops; free copper on an unused layer tips it to a pour. Make the per-rail choice explicit from these three facts. Test: classify rails on open boards and compare with their IR and thermal performance.
- **XD43 Loops for moving loads** (combo, M). Leaf venation forms loops when loads fluctuate or links fail (Katifori, Szöllősi, Magnasco 2010; Corson 2010) [S]. When load current moves (FPGA regions, motor phases, multiphase interleave), optimize the copper over the set of load scenarios; meshes appear where they pay. Test: worst-scenario drop of a tree vs a scenario-optimized mesh at equal copper.
- **XD44 Physarum and Hu–Cai adaptive copper growth** (new for PCB, M). Iterate: solve currents on a fine conductance grid for the load scenarios, grow each conductance as |I|^μ minus a decay, and renormalize to a copper budget. Tero et al. (Science 2010) [S] built Tokyo-rail-like networks this way. Bonifaci et al. proved that Physarum dynamics minimizes network cost plus routing cost [S]. It is cheap, local, parallel and GPU-friendly, and can seed or replace SIMP. Test: loss and drop vs SIMP at equal area on workstream 6's benchmark.
- **XD45 Constructal area-to-point collectors** (sharp, M). Bejan's constructal trees (1997) [S] size hierarchical collectors that gather flow from an area. Use them where many distributed loads or sources share a layer with other nets: LED arrays, paralleled cells, distributed power pins. Compare against a full pour when the layer is free. Test: drop and copper use vs a pour on a 64-LED array.
- **XD46 Tapered collectors** (sharp, S). Solar-cell busbars widen as they collect current. Where current accumulates along a conductor (a row of FETs, a LED strip, battery tabs), make width ∝ accumulated current. [C] that gives 25% less loss at equal copper; or keep the loss and free about 25% of the area for routing. Test: FD on a 10-FET row.
- **XD47 Reverse-return (Tichelmann) feeding** (sharp, S). Hydronic heating feeds radiators from one end and returns from the other, so every path has the same length. Do the same for paralleled loads. [C] 8 elements with a rail-segment/element resistance ratio of 0.1: max/min current share 13.6 when fed and returned at the same end, 2.4 when the return leaves from the far end. With 4 elements: 2.4 vs 1.2. Use it for paralleled FETs, LED strings, battery cells and multiphase outputs. Test: SPICE plus FD on the copper.
- **XD48 HVAC duct-sizing modes as named rail strategies** (sharp, S). HVAC has three standard duct-sizing methods. Each maps to a rail-sizing strategy the LLM can state, which the solver then executes:

  | HVAC method | Copper meaning |
  |---|---|
  | equal friction | constant mV per mm, i.e. w ∝ I |
  | static regain | equal voltage at every load |
  | velocity reduction | J limits |

  Test: implement all three in [xd_power_tree.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_power_tree.py).
- **XD49 Surge tank sizing (water hammer ↔ hot-plug ringing)** (sharp, S). Joukowsky's ΔP = ρ·c·Δv corresponds to ΔV = Z0·ΔI on a long input cable. The surge tank is a damped bulk capacitor. Size it from cable L and the ceramic input C so damping ζ ≥ 0.5, instead of "add a 47 µF electrolytic". Test: SPICE hot-plug overshoot vs ζ.
- **XD50 DC load flow with N-1 contingency** (new, S–M). Grids must survive any single line outage. Check that losing any one via, neck or connector pin (a void, a crack, a cold joint) does not push the remaining copper past its current or thermal limit. Test: fault-inject single vias on 5 high-current boards.
- **XD51 Rank-1 what-if updates (PTDF/LODF analogy)** (sharp, S). Grid operators update flows after an outage without re-solving. Factor the copper conductance matrix once, then score deleting a via, adding a slot or necking a pour by Sherman–Morrison or Woodbury low-rank updates. That makes N-1 (XD50) and the live HUD (U1) fast. Test: timing and accuracy vs re-solve on a 200k-node sheet.
- **XD52 Protection coordination for copper (I²t)** (new, S). Cable sizing checks short-circuit withstand, k²S² ≥ I²t (IEC 60949 form) [S]. Every trace downstream of a fuse, eFuse or current limiter should withstand that device's let-through I²t. [C] 1 oz traces from 25 to 180 °C withstand 1.2 A²s at 0.2 mm, 7.3 at 0.5 mm, 29 at 1 mm and 264 at 3 mm. Compare with the fuse datasheet I²t. Test: audit fused rails on 20 open boards.
- **XD53 Ground-grid step voltage → equipotential placement** (new, S). Substation grounding limits the voltage a person bridges across the ground (IEEE Std 80) [S]. On a pour, a point return injection makes a radial gradient E = R_s·I/(2πr). [C] 10 A injected 10 mm from a 5 mm ADC footprint drops 400 µV across it, which is 8 LSB at 16 bit and 3.3 V. Placed tangentially (on an equipotential), the drop is ≈ 0. Orient sensitive ground-pin pairs along equipotentials, or steer the return stream away. Test: FD on 5 mixed-signal boards; bench measurement.
- **XD54 Horton–Strahler orders for power trees** (combo, S). River networks label each branch by order. Label power-tree segments the same way, so rules and LLM intents can say "order-3 trunk on L3, order-1 twigs on top" without listing nets. Test: does the label predict the width and layer choices on good open boards?
- **XD55 Harmonic load flow on copper** (sharp, M). Grids compute harmonic flows. Solve the complex sheet problem (R_s + jω·μ0·d per square) at each switching harmonic to map where ripple currents actually flow in pours, for example under an ADC. Test: compare with near-field scans of a buck board.
- **XD56 Copper's shadow price** (combo, S). The Lagrange multiplier on the copper-area constraint in XD39 is the marginal value of 1 mm² of copper in a region, in mW or mV. Publish it per region to the resource market N1, so routing and power negotiate in physical units. Test: price stability across re-solves.
- **XD57 Kelvin's law for battery products** (sharp, S). Kelvin sized conductors where the marginal cost of copper equals the marginal cost of lifetime losses. For battery devices, loss is runtime. Size the main rails where a mm² of copper (or one layer) costs the same as the runtime it buys. Test: a worked example on an open battery board.
- **XD58 H-tree feeds for paralleled loads** (sharp, S). Clock H-trees give equal path lengths. Use H-tree or reverse-return (XD47) topologies for paralleled devices that must share current or switch together. Test: FD current share vs a comb feed.

## F. Structures: topology optimization, mechanics, additive manufacturing

- **XD59 Shear lag for every overlapping joint** (sharp, S). The XD22 math covers wide-terminal solder joints, bolted busbar lugs, overlapping pours on adjacent layers and thermal vias under distributed sources. Overlap beyond about 2–3 L_T adds almost nothing. Test: FD sweep of overlap length.
- **XD60 Stress-concentration charts as current-crowding charts** (new, S). Laplace current flow around an insulating hole matches potential flow. A round hole in uniform current has J_max = 2·J0; an elliptical hole with semi-axis a across the flow and b along it has J_max = J0·(1 + a/b) [I, standard potential-flow results]. Peterson-style charts then give closed-form hot-spot checks around mounting holes, antipads and slots. Test: FD vs formula for 10 hole shapes.
- **XD61 Align copper texture with current (Michell analogy)** (combo, S). Michell trusses follow principal stress lines. Align hatch, slots, via rows and thermal-relief spokes with current streamlines (ψ contours) so the texture costs nothing electrically. Test: XD2 cost of a hatch at 0°, 45° and 90° to the flow.
- **XD62 Multi-load-case copper** (combo, S–M). Structural TO handles many load cases. Optimize copper over operating modes (light load, full load, transient, fault, standby) with a weighted or worst-case objective. Test: worst-case drop vs a single-case design.
- **XD63 Steinberg displacement limits for placement** (sharp, S–M). Aerospace vibration practice limits board deflection under a part by its size and position (Steinberg) [S]. Use a plate modal model to place large, brittle or heavy parts (MLCCs, BGAs, crystals, transformers) at low-curvature points, and orient MLCCs along low-strain axes. Test: plate FE on 5 boards with their mounting holes.
- **XD64 Warpage from copper imbalance** (sharp, S). Composite laminate theory predicts bow and twist from per-layer copper density asymmetry about the mid-plane. Add a laminate-theory warpage critic and balance pours to pass it. Test: predicted vs fab-reported bow.
- **XD65 Neutral-axis flex routing** (sharp, S). In laminates, strain = y/R. Put flex traces at the neutral axis, perpendicular to the bend, staggered across layers. This is known; make it computed from the bend radius. Test: strain estimate vs IPC-2223 guidance.
- **XD66 Manufacturing filters double as DFM** (sharp, S). AM overhang and minimum-feature filters correspond to PCB slivers, minimum area and minimum annular ring. Run them as a filter on optimized copper. Acid traps are mostly a legacy concern with modern etching, so keep that rule soft.

## G. Harness, aerospace, automotive, audio and instrumentation practice

- **XD67 Separation tables derived from coupling** (sharp, S). Harness standards assign wire classes and separation distances. Derive the PCB equivalent per aggressor–victim pair from a coupling kernel and a dB budget rather than a fixed mm. [I] crosstalk over a plane falls as 1/(1+(d/h)²). Test: derived vs fixed tables on 10 boards.
- **XD68 Buffer distance from multipole order** (new, S). In acoustics, point sources decay 6 dB per doubling and line sources 3 dB. On boards, the order of the aggressor sets the decay:

  | Aggressor | Coupling falls as | Far-field slope |
  |---|---|---|
  | parallel lines over a plane | 1/(d/h)² | −12 dB/doubling |
  | a free dipole loop | 1/r³ | −18 dB/doubling |
  | flat loops over a plane | h²/r⁵ | −30 dB/doubling |

  [C] flat 3 mm loops 0.5 mm over a plane couple 26 dB less than in free space at 10 mm, and 50 dB less at 40 mm. Standing loops (axis parallel to the board) are doubled, not cancelled, by the plane. Compute buffer distance from the order and the budget. Test: Neumann vs openEMS for 4 geometries.
- **XD69 Ground-tap ordering on a shared return** (sharp, S). Audio amplifier practice orders ground taps: the most sensitive reference nearest the star point, and the largest currents joining last (Self) [S]. Solve it as an assignment that minimizes Σ (current × shared impedance × victim sensitivity). Test: harmonic distortion in SPICE for best vs worst order on a class-AB layout.
- **XD70 Pin-1 rule as a stream check** (sharp, S). The "pin-1 problem" is cable shield current flowing into circuit ground inside the box (Muncy, JAES 1995) [S]. Check with the stream function that no chassis or shield current streamline crosses the analog ground region. Test: seeded violations on 5 audio boards.
- **XD71 Orient magnetics by dipole algebra** (new, S). Dipole coupling ∝ m1·m2 − 3(m1·r̂)(m2·r̂). [C] two in-plane-axis dipoles null at 54.7° (the NMR "magic angle"): M = 3.75 pH at 0°, 0.0003 pH at 54.7°, −2.0 pH at 90°, at 20 mm. A flat victim beside an in-plane-axis aggressor at the same height couples only through the plane's image. Choose inductor and loop orientations from the formula, not "rotate 90°". Test: near-field measurement of a drum-core inductor vs a sense loop at 5 angles.
- **XD72 Current-free sense paths (Kelvin generalized)** (sharp, S). Every voltage that matters (feedback, shunt sense, reference, ADC ground) must have a sense path that carries no load current. The DC solve verifies it: the sense conductor's |J| is ≈ 0 under load. Test: seeded violations.
- **XD73 Lightning protection zones → board protection zones** (sharp, S). IEC 62305 nests protection zones and bonds or filters every conductor at each zone boundary [S]. Draw the board's zones (connector, filtered, quiet core) and check that every crossing conductor passes a filter or bond at the boundary line. Test: ESD and surge paths on 10 interface boards.
- **XD74 Bond-impedance budgets** (sharp, S). Aerospace bonding specifies the bond's resistance (a 2.5 mΩ class R bond in MIL-B-5087 practice) [S]. Give board-to-chassis bonds (screws, standoffs, spring fingers, stitched vias) a nH budget at the frequency of concern, computed from geometry. Test: PEEC of 3 mounting-hole styles.
- **XD75 Ground-offset budgets between blocks** (sharp, S). Automotive networks specify tolerated ground offsets between modules. Give each pair of interconnected blocks on a board a DC and AC ground-offset budget in mV, checked by the copper solve. Test: offsets on 5 mixed-signal boards.
- **XD76 Twisted and gradiometric sense traces** (sharp, S). Twisted pairs and gradiometer pickup coils cancel uniform-field pickup. Route low-level differential sense lines near magnetics with layer swaps every segment, or as figure-8 loops. Pickup then falls to the residual imbalance. Test: Neumann M from an inductor into a straight vs a twisted sense pair.
- **XD77 Shim loops (NMR shimming analogy)** (new, S–M). Shim coils null residual field errors. After layout, add a small compensating loop to a sense path, sized and oriented to cancel the computed residual M from a nearby aggressor. Test: simulate residual M before and after; near-field verify.

## H. Cavities, metamaterials and acoustics

- **XD78 Damp cavity modes at corners and edges, like bass traps** (sharp, S). Room-mode pressure antinodes sit in corners. Plane-pair voltage antinodes sit at open edges and corners. Put lossy damping (RC edge terminations, lossy-ESR capacitors) there, weighted by modal participation. This is known PDN practice ([decoupling-pdn](../../topics/rules/decoupling-pdn.md)); the transplant adds computed placement. Test: cavity Q with vs without corner damping.
- **XD79 EBG only for islands** (sharp, M). Planar EBG power planes give wide stop bands [S], but they perforate the return path, hurt SI and can radiate [S]. Use them only to isolate a noisy island (PA, clock) from a sensitive one with no fast signal crossing. Test: isolation vs eye opening on a shared-plane test board.
- **XD80 Hatched planes by wire-mesh shield theory** (sharp, S). Treat a hatched or meshed plane (flex, rigid-flex) as a wire-mesh screen with an effective sheet impedance and transfer impedance. Predict the Z0 ripple and return inductance from pitch and fill. Test: 2-D field solve of microstrip over a hatch.
- **XD81 Tuned resonant absorbers** (sharp, S–M). Acoustic Helmholtz absorbers target one room mode. A lossy stub or series RC tuned to one cavity or enclosure mode, placed at its antinode, does the same. Test: S21 between two cavity ports.
- **XD82 Finite guard edges and diffraction** (new, S). Noise barriers leak around their ends (Maekawa). A guard trace or via fence of finite length has the same end leakage, so score guards by how much aggressor field reaches the victim around the ends, not by their existence. Test: 2-D/3-D solve of guard length vs isolation.

## I. Superconducting quantum chip layout

- **XD83 Stitching spacing from slot-mode resonance (airbridge rule)** (sharp, S). Airbridges tie CPW grounds; spacing below a quarter wavelength pushes slotline resonances above the band (Chen et al. 2014; strong-coupling waveguide work) [S]. Apply this to CPWG via stitching and to ground pour fragments on both sides of any slot or trace. Spacing comes from the slot's resonance against the aggressor spectrum, not from λ/20. Test: slot-mode S21 for 3 spacings.
- **XD84 Energy participation ratio as a layout metric** (new, M). The EPR is the fraction of a mode's energy stored in an element or region, from one eigenmode solve (Minev et al., npj QI 2021) [S]. Use participation in solder mask and FR-4 (loss), in a neighbour's region (coupling) or near a lid (detuning) to rank sensitivity. Test: EPR vs measured insertion loss of 3 trace variants.
- **XD85 Box modes early** (sharp, S). Quantum chips are checked for package modes before layout. Compute the TE_mn0 modes of shield cans and enclosures up front; place strong harmonics off them or add absorber or partitions. [C] a 30×20 mm can resonates at 9.0 GHz, a 200×100 mm box at 1.68 GHz. Test: harmonic table vs can modes on 5 RF boards.
- **XD86 Crossovers keep the ground continuous** (sharp, S). Quantum chips route crossovers on airbridges that leave the ground plane intact. On a PCB, a 0 Ω jumper or a bottom-layer crossover for a fast net needs a parallel ground bridge (stitching vias on both sides). Check it with return-path continuity. Test: R2 shadow-return check on 0 Ω jumpers.
- **XD87 Component-plus-analysis architecture** (combo, M). Qiskit Metal (Apache-2.0 [S]) and KQCircuits (GPL-3.0 [S]) attach physics analyses (EPR, lumped-oscillator) to parametric components. Build PCB physics primitives (hot-loop cell, busbar, via cage) the same way: code that emits geometry plus its own analysis hook. Test: three primitives with analyses.

## J. Biology

- **XD88 Windkessel hierarchy → damped PDN hierarchy** (sharp, S). The arterial tree puts compliance (elastic arteries) and resistance (arterioles) at the right levels to damp pulsatile flow. In a PDN, put controlled ESR (or a resistive link) at the bulk-to-local transition to flatten anti-resonances, rather than adding more capacitors. Test: impedance profile with vs without controlled-ESR parts.
- **XD89 Neurovascular co-routing** (new framing, S). Nerves and vessels run together because nothing couples them. Separate nets only where a coupling mechanism exists (dI/dt, dV/dt, shared impedance, thermal). So slow DC control lines may share a corridor with a DC power trunk, but not with a switch node. Test: how many separation rules on 10 boards are not backed by a computed mechanism.
- **XD90 Myelin and nodes of Ranvier** (sharp, S). Insulation plus periodic regeneration maps to low-Dk routing plus repeaters. Place retimers and redrivers at budgeted loss intervals, not mid-way by habit. Test: channel loss budget per segment.
- **XD91 Wiring economy as a benchmark** (combo, S). C. elegans ganglia placement minimizes wire among many permutations (Cherniak 1994) [S]. For a good human layout, count how many random permutations of its blocks beat it on physics cost. This measures whether our cost function agrees with good practice. Test: 10 boards, 10⁴ permutations each.
- **XD92 Reticulate pours** (combo, M). Leaves combine main veins with a fine loop mesh for damage tolerance. Use a trunk plus mesh for very large high-current pours that must survive voids and cracks (with N-1, XD50). Test: worst-case drop after random void injection, trunk vs trunk + mesh.
- **XD93 Developmental pruning** (combo, S). Hu–Cai adaptation models developmental pruning [S]. Remove low-|J| copper only when another net needs the room, and give it back afterward. Test: freed area vs loss increase (see the greedy trim in [power-copper](../../topics/physics/power-copper.md)).

## K. Computer graphics and computational geometry

- **XD94 SDF clearance and physics on GPU** (sharp, S–M). Signed distance fields per net give exact clearance, push-apart gradients and cheap distance-based coupling kernels in one texture. Test: speed and accuracy vs polygon DRC on 5 boards.
- **XD95 Medial-axis neck finder** (new, S). The medial-axis radius along a pour gives its local width. The minimum along each high-|J| path (from ψ) is the bottleneck. Flag necks by current carried over width. Test: neck flags vs FD hot spots.
- **XD96 Arcs over clothoids** (sharp, S). The offset of an arc is an arc, so pair spacing and clearance stay exact with line-and-arc (G1) geometry. A clothoid's offset is not a clothoid. Curvature continuity (G2) matters for aesthetics and mm-wave, not below several GHz. [C] a sharp 90° corner counts as 0.56 squares at DC, less than its centreline length. Use arcs where curves are wanted. Test: pair-spacing error of offset clothoids vs arcs.
- **XD97 Discrete elastic rods for trace relaxation** (combo, M). Model traces as Kirchhoff rods (Bergou et al. 2008) [S] with tension (length), bending stiffness (curvature) and SDF repulsion, plus physics penalties. They relax to smooth, beautiful, clearance-respecting curves: a "rubber band with stiffness" router post-pass. Test: aesthetic judge and DRC on relaxed vs raw routes.
- **XD98 Streamline-aligned direction fields** (combo, M). Cross-field design (Instant Meshes) builds smooth direction fields. Seed them from current streamlines in power regions and from signal flow elsewhere, as preferred directions for routing. Test: via count and crossing count vs no field.
- **XD99 Blue-noise stitching vias** (new, S). Maximal Poisson-disk sampling guarantees a minimum spacing (DRC) and a maximum empty circle (the physical leakage or resonance metric), around arbitrary obstacles. It replaces grids that break at obstacles. Test: largest empty circle and via count vs grid stitching.
- **XD100 Weighted centroidal Voronoi allocation** (combo, S). Lloyd relaxation weighted by load-current density spaces decaps or return vias so each serves equal current. Cap the cells at the screening length (XD112). Test: worst-pin impedance vs uniform spacing.
- **XD101 Geodesic creepage** (sharp, S). Creepage is a surface geodesic around slots and edges. The heat method (Crane et al. 2013) [S] or fast marching computes it exactly on a 2-D mesh. Test: against hand measurements on 10 HV boards.
- **XD102 Differentiable copper rasterization** (new, M). A soft rasterizer maps polygon vertices to a density grid, so physics gradients flow back to CAD polygons rather than pixels. Test: gradient check; convergence vs SIMP-then-polygonize.
- **XD103 Contour-styled outlines** (new, S). Where a pour edge has no physics cost (low |J|, slow node), snap it to smoothed equipotential or stream contours. The board then looks designed and reveals its current flow at zero physics cost. Test: VLM aesthetic preference.

## L. Game physics and robotics

- **XD104 XPBD interactive placement** (sharp, M). Extended position-based dynamics (Macklin et al. 2016) [S] solves constraints by projection in real time. Use clearance (inequality), maximum hot-loop length (distance), symmetry and alignment as constraints, with stiffness from budget slack. Drag-and-feel physics for U1. Test: frame rate and budget violations during drag on a 200-part board.
- **XD105 CHOMP/TrajOpt trace shaping** (combo, M). CHOMP minimizes a trajectory functional (obstacle cost from SDF plus smoothness); TrajOpt adds hard constraints by SQP [S]. Use coupling, reference-gap and clearance costs plus curvature on critical traces, after topology is fixed. Test: coupling and length vs the router's output.
- **XD106 Dubins primitives for minimum bend radius** (sharp, S). Shortest curvature-bounded paths are closed-form arcs and lines. Use them for flex, RF and high-voltage traces with a minimum radius. Test: router integration on 3 flex boards.
- **XD107 Broad-phase coupling search** (sharp, S). Game engines find nearby pairs with BVHs and spatial hashing. Find aggressor–victim pairs inside a coupling radius set by the kernel and dB budget, in O(n log n). Test: pairs found vs brute force.

## M. Multiscale modelling, sparse fields, inverse problems (weather, climate, geophysics)

- **XD108 Subgrid homogenization of perforated planes** (sharp, S). Climate models parameterize what the grid cannot resolve. Represent BGA via fields and antipad "Swiss cheese" by an anisotropic effective sheet-conductance tensor per pattern, so board-level solves stay coarse ([power-copper](../../topics/physics/power-copper.md) computes the scalar case). Test: homogenized vs fine solve.
- **XD109 Nested grids** (sharp, M). Regional models nest inside global ones. Run a coarse board solve, then fine local solves (hot loop, BGA escape) using its boundary values, with two-way feedback. Test: error vs a fully fine solve at 10× less cost.
- **XD110 Tolerance ensembles** (sharp, M). Weather forecasts run ensembles. Run each physics check over an ensemble of fab, material and part variations (Dk, etch, ESL, ESR, registration) and report the spread; robust layouts minimize the worst member. Test: how often the nominal pass fails in the ensemble.
- **XD111 Data assimilation for model calibration** (combo, M). 4D-Var fits model parameters to observations by adjoint. Fit stackup Dk, roughness, via ESL and h coefficients to TDR, VNA and thermal-camera data from built boards, and feed them back as priors. Test: prediction error before and after on a second board.
- **XD112 Physical screening kernels in an ePlace-style placer** (new, M). ePlace (Lu et al., TODAES 2015) [S] treats density as charge and solves Poisson by FFT. Replace that density potential with real physics kernels, all FFT-convolvable:

  | Physics | Kernel | [C] length scale |
  |---|---|---|
  | Thermal | K0(r/λ_th) | λ_th = 19–64 mm |
  | PDN decap reach | K0(r/λ_pdn) | λ_pdn = 4–24 mm |
  | Magnetic | dipole 1/r³, or h²/r⁵ over a plane | — |
  | Crosstalk | 1/(1+(d/h)²) | — |

  Test: physics-budget violations vs an HPWL plus density placer on 10 boards.
- **XD113 Decap reach set by a screening length** (new, S). A plane pair (μ0·d per square) with n decaps per m² of mounting inductance L_c obeys a screened Laplace equation with Debye-like length λ = sqrt(L_c/(μ0·d·n)), in the regime where the decaps are inductive. The share of load current from decaps within radius R is 1 − x·K1(x), with x = R/λ. [C] for d = 0.2 mm and 10 mm decap pitch, λ = 16.7 mm. The lattice FD gives 22/42/66/88% within 0.5/1/2/3 λ, against 17/40/72/88% predicted. "Within λ/2" replaces "as close as possible", and it says when location matters: thick cavities. Test: extend [xd_lengths.py](../../../tools/research/physics/xd_lengths.py) with plane capacitance and compare with a PDN tool.
- **XD114 Thermal fin length as a pour-size rule** (sharp, S). Fin theory gives λ_th = sqrt(Σk·t/h). [C] a 2-layer board in still air has λ = 37 mm, and a pour radius of 55 mm already reaches 90% of the infinite-plate conductance for a 5 mm source. 4-layer: λ = 52 mm, 90% at 74 mm. Bigger pours buy little; parts closer than about λ heat each other. Test: thermal FV (workstream 7) vs the formula.
- **XD115 Compressed-sensing near-field scans** (new, M). Reconstruct the board's H-field from a few probe points using sparsity in a dipole basis. This validates the physics model on built boards cheaply. Test: reconstruction error vs a dense scan.
- **XD116 Near-to-far-field transformation** (sharp, S–M). Antenna ranges and potential-field geophysics transform measured or simulated near fields to far fields. Do this from board currents to estimate 3 m emissions early. Test: vs openEMS far field.
- **XD117 Thermal-image source inversion** (combo, M). Inverse heat conduction recovers heat sources from an IR image. Use it to check that the copper's predicted dissipation map matches the built board. Test: one board, known loads.
- **XD118 Resistivity tomography for copper defects** (new, M). Geophysical ERT infers conductivity from boundary voltages. Use 4-wire voltages at test points to detect plating voids and cracked necks in power copper. Test: seeded-void boards.
- **XD119 Implicit neural fields for copper and potential** (new, L). SIREN-style continuous representations give resolution-free, differentiable copper and field functions for multiscale optimization. Test: accuracy and speed vs grids at equal parameters.

## N. City and transport planning

- **XD120 Road hierarchy for current** (combo, S). Arterials, collectors and local streets map to inner planes and wide pours, branches, and twigs. Forbid "through traffic", meaning a large return crossing a quiet neighbourhood, and use calming measures (slots placed along streamlines, XD3) to divert it. Test: ψ check of large returns crossing quiet zones.
- **XD121 Zones defined by current loops, not net names (sewer separation)** (new framing, S). Separate sewers only work when each flow stays in its own pipes. A zone is quiet if no noisy current's streamline (at the relevant frequency) crosses it, whatever the net is named. This replaces split-ground folklore with a check. Test: zone audit with ψ on 10 mixed-signal boards.
- **XD122 Pour-everything only where Rayleigh allows** (new framing, S). Rayleigh's monotonicity law says adding copper never raises DC resistance, so filling is free for quiet nets. Switch nodes are different: more area is more capacitance and more antenna. [C] a 10×10 mm switch-node pour 0.2 mm over ground is about 19 pF and injects 0.38 A at 20 V/ns. Policy: maximize copper on low-dV/dt nets (VIN, GND, VOUT), minimize it on high-dV/dt nodes. This answers the user's "closer to a pour than a trace" with a regime. Test: switch-node area vs conducted and radiated emissions in simulation.

## O. Electrochemistry, batteries, solar

- **XD123 Plating thickness from the primary current distribution** (sharp, S–M). Electroplating follows Laplace in the bath, so isolated outer-layer features plate thicker (Wagner number). - **XD124 Multi-tab feeding** (sharp, S). Large-format cells use several tabs to even out current density. Feed large pours from several entry points spread along the edge, with positions chosen to minimize peak |J| and IR drop. Test: FD on a 30 A pour, 1 vs 3 entries.
- **XD125 Solar-grid finger optimization for shared layers** (combo, S). Solar grids trade shading against resistive loss. Copper on a shared layer has an opportunity cost (routing space), like shading. Optimize finger pitch and width of comb-like collectors with that price (XD56). Test: loss vs freed routing area.
