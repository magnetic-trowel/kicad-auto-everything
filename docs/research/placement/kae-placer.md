# kae placer: CP-SAT floorplan and compiled annealing

Scope: defensive publication of `kae place` (seed, macros, CP-SAT floorplan, compiled annealer, grid legalization), its runs and open problems. Not here: exact legality and KiCad loop -> [exact legality](kae-exact-legality.md); stage → module map -> [kae place hub](../../topics/placement/kae-place.md).
Keys: kae place, quadratic seed, clique model, partition_spread, physics macros, clusters.py, no-good cuts, Terms.breakdown, CP-SAT floorplan, FLOORPLAN_MAX_ITEMS, FastAnnealer, SA_U_SAT, w_phys, w_wl, w_cong, w_disp, w_order, w_label, w_silk, grid legalization, own/opp/board rectangles, _fastlegal.py, csweep.py, physics ablation, routability probe, dense sensor boards

**Status:** worked.
**First published:** 2026-10-22, commit [64a6858](https://github.com/magnetic-trowel/kicad-auto-everything/commit/64a6858cca92b72ead01cdf56e3b5667a71cb2bc) on branch `claude/vigilant-mayer-i91rrr`.

`kae place` places a KiCad board from its netlist: a quadratic seed, physics macros micro-placed by CP-SAT, a CP-SAT floorplan, a numba simulated annealer pricing wirelength, congestion, legality and the [fast physics terms](kae-physics-terms.md), then grid and exact legalization closed by KiCad's own DRC. On split C (79 boards of the qualified suite) it is legal on 72/72 tasks in a median of about 2 minutes, and its physics score is 1,410 against 35,380 without the physics terms and 4,392 for the human layouts (kae's own uncalibrated estimate). Routing our placements is a different story: see [kae-staged](../joint/kae-staged.md).

## Idea and how it works

Pipeline (`kae/src/kae/place/pipeline.py`): Problem -> [brief](kae-comprehension-brief.md) -> macros -> seed -> anneal -> legalize -> exact closed loop -> order pass -> silk pass -> KiCad DRC loop (<= 6 rounds). The last four steps are on [kae-exact-legality](kae-exact-legality.md). Time budget: finish within min(0.85 x time - 10 s, 20 s + 3 s per movable part); the annealer counts CPU seconds, CP-SAT wall seconds with `--workers` threads. Parts keep their side (no side flips).

**1. Quadratic seed.** Object centres minimize weighted squared spring length (clique model, weight w/(pins - 1); ground nets x0.1, power x0.2; clock, high-speed and RF x1.5, analog x1.2; brief structure nets up-weighted). Locked parts anchor through their pins; connectors are pulled to the nearest board edge; a weak spring pulls to the centre.

**2. Spreading.** Pairwise rectangle repulsion with the springs still active, or on big boards `partition_spread`: recursive bisection of free cells per side in proportion to area.

**3. Physics macros** (`clusters.py`). Brief structures become segments whose L1 length is a geometric proxy (decap: cap rail pad <-> paired IC power pin, cap ground <-> nearest IC ground; crystal: pads <-> XIN/XOUT, load caps <-> crystal; cell: consecutive loop members, closed; chain: consecutive members from the connector pin). Structures sharing parts are unioned (IC + decaps + crystal) and anchored on the largest or locked member. CP-SAT micro-places the members (4 rotations, NoOverlap2D per side, minimize weighted segment length + compactness) and enumerates distinct layouts with no-good cuts on the rotation assignment. The layouts are ranked by the real physics terms (`Terms.breakdown`), the best is annealed on them ("the proxy proposes, the physics decides"), and the best K are kept for the floorplan and for the annealer's re-layout move.

**4. CP-SAT floorplan** (`cpsat.py`). Items are parts or rigid macros, each with variants (rotation x internal layout) as a one-hot choice; optional interval variables per member rectangle (own side; drilled pads and back courtyards on the other side); one NoOverlap2D per side including fixed obstacles; the outline as containment in one of the problem's cover rectangles. Objective: L1 displacement from the spread positions + a rotation-change charge + weighted HPWL. Used up to `FLOORPLAN_MAX_ITEMS = 40` items (code comment: above this it "rarely finds a solution in time"; no measurement is recorded); bigger boards rely on bisection spreading. Legalization windows and the all-parts model are capped at 40 too (`legalize.MAX_WINDOW`, `GLOBAL_MAX`). The solver-bench sizes (monolithic CP-SAT feasible to ~200 parts, full-board legalizer to ~300; [benchmarks](../../topics/solvers/benchmarks.md)) were measured on synthetic instances (one courtyard rectangle per part, mostly one side, 0.5 mm grid) and do not carry over to this model's macros, layout variants and sliced rectangles.

**5. Annealer** (`engine.py`, `anneal.py`, `cost.py`). Cost = w_phys * physics (utilization, linear, saturated at `SA_U_SAT = 3` per structure) + w_wl * HPWL / seed HPWL (+ w_cong * RUDY overflow) + lambda(t) * legality (grid overlap area own side / opposite side / obstacles + area outside the outline; lambda ramped geometrically) + w_disp * L1 displacement from the seed + w_order * protection-order violation + w_label * label distance + w_silk * soft silk overlap. Moves: shift with a radius that shrinks with temperature, rotate in place, swap equal or similar-size footprints, macro shift and rotate, macro re-layout from the top K. Ground and power nets with > 40 pins are frozen out of HPWL. `FastAnnealer` (numba) keeps pin coordinates, per-net bounding boxes, a per-side bucket hash of rectangles, an outline summed-area table, RUDY demand per bin and the order rules incrementally. Non-physics parts move entirely inside compiled batches; physics and macro moves are proposed in Python, priced by `Terms.delta` and evaluated by the same compiled move function; aggressor-loop members are sampled at half rate. The schedule runs over a planned move count, so results are reproducible when the budget does not bind. Engine state is passed by value and every extra field costs move rate (12 fields ~ 10%), so label and silk data are packed into two arrays. The Python `Annealer` stays as reference and fallback.

**6. Grid legalization** (`problem.py`, `legalize.py`, `_fastlegal.py`). `Problem` turns each movable part, per rotation, into rectangles: `own` (courtyard box grown to cover pads inflated by half the clearance, so disjoint `own` rectangles imply legal courtyards and pad clearance), `opp` (drilled pads and back courtyard), `board` (pads inflated by edge clearance, plus courtyard), and pins, all rounded outward to the grid g, so grid-legal implies legal. g is the finest of `GRID_LADDER` = 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.25 mm that keeps the board's longer span within 1,000 cells (0.25 mm beyond 250 mm; `Problem._domain`): outward rounding wastes at most one cell per edge, so a fine grid keeps dense boards packable, while the cell cap bounds the CP-SAT domains. `legalize` groups nearby violators, frees each group plus its k nearest movable parts in a window and minimizes L1 displacement + rotation charge with CP-SAT, growing windows (k x 1.6) on failure and finally freeing everything. The numba greedy fallback keeps a per-side summed-area occupancy table (O(1) rectangle queries) and finds the nearest free origin by L1 rings. The annealed result is kept only if grid-legal after legalization and not costlier than the legal seed.

**Routing margin** (`Problem.auto_margin`, `set_margin`). Every movable part's occupancy grows by m/2 per side, with m = min(1, max(0, 2 x (0.7 - utilization))) mm: about 1 mm on an empty board and nothing above 70% utilization. Parts get m x min(0.8, 0.1 sqrt(pins)) more for pin escape, and up to track + clearance more towards the edge. The margin is a wish: it is the first thing dropped when parts do not fit (`_escalate`, final legality). The formula appeared in a WIP snapshot ([1120fc0](https://github.com/magnetic-trowel/kicad-auto-everything/commit/1120fc039a1dbb970fe7f05bfe731f6513094e5d)) with no measurement, and it has never been ablated. The nearest test is the pnr control `seq_margin`, an a-priori margin scaled by pin count that showed no merit and cost legality on dense boards ([sequential-controls](../joint/sequential-controls.md)). kae's version vanishes on dense boards, so that verdict does not carry over directly. It is **not** the BGA rework clearance in [dfm-fab-limits](../../topics/rules/dfm-fab-limits.md) (1.0 mm courtyard, ~1.5 mm rework). That is an assembly keep-out, and the footprint's courtyard carries it: KiCad's library convention KLC F5.3 draws BGA courtyards 1.0 mm out and connectors 0.5 mm, against 0.25 mm by default. KiCad's courtyard DRC then enforces it, and so does kae's legality. `place_margin` (-1 = auto; >= 0 fixes m) is now 0 by default. The A/B placement-decide-staged (213 merit boards, k = 2) found auto and 0 equal (cp1 0.305 both, completion 0.942 / 0.941), so the formula with no source was dropped.

The seed and the macros were not ablated separately; probe-select runs showed the floorplan pulls jittered seeds back together, so seed jitter barely diversifies candidates.

## Open problems

- Dense sensor boards (paw3395, badjeff-pmw3610, siderakb-pmw3610): our placements put caps in the strips between the sensor's pins and the edge; Freerouting's first pass then runs ~500 s vs 13–155 s on the human's. With the connectors fixed, paw3395 drops to 2 unconnected on one routing. No estimate predicts these failures; the routing probe is the only, noisy, signal ([routability estimators](kae-routability-estimators.md)).
- Split-C routability: 15 boards route clean only on the human placement ([pipeline C](../../../bench/results/2026-10-24-pipeline-c-c9c49d6.md)). Locally, 4 of 8 re-run boards routed clean on ours, 2 failed only on silk (since fixed), 2 failed on routing: eurorack (HPWL 0.76× human, crowded) and sdtv2csi (HPWL 1.22×). The 4-way routing portfolio may close some ([portfolio](../../../bench/results/2026-10-24-portfolio-ab-0465818.md)).
- Other findings: pingdevkit's `GetRectangleWidth` assert is printed by pcbnew's zone fill on the human boards too (the split-C score error was the pcbnew process ending, not our writer). Probe DRC errors on split C: silk errors were placement errors that survived (fixed by the turned-glyph model); the others (mask bridge, hole and edge clearance) did not appear on the 8 boards re-run locally.

## Verdict

Worked as a placer: legal on every split-C task with the KiCad loop, physics about 3x better than the human layouts and 25x better than without the terms, with no routability cost on A+B. What it does not do is make boards easier to route: on split C, 30/78 of our placements route clean against 43/78 human ones ([kae-staged](../joint/kae-staged.md)), and no estimator we tried predicts which placements fail ([kae-routability-estimators](kae-routability-estimators.md)). The physics score is uncalibrated and single-seed. `kae place` alone is not a merit method; it runs inside both "kae staged (restarts)" and "kae joint loop (restarts)".
